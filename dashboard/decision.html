ASSET A · NET LEASE RESEARCH● Evidence snapshot · 2026-11-08

Decision pathways

Four realistic paths. This page states what each one depends on and what has to be true for it to work. It does not rank them with a score, because a score would require probabilities and prices that no official source supplies.

Why there is no winner declared here

Earlier layers of this repository published numerical rankings of these pathways, and they disagreed with each other. They disagreed because each one quietly assumed a rent, a cap rate and a renewal probability. None of those was ever supplied. A ranking built that way is not analysis; it is a guess with decimal places. The drivers are laid out below so that you can weight them yourself once the blocker documents are in hand.

Option A — Sell before expiry

Convert an illiquid, single-tenant, single-site asset into cash or into a diversified position while a lease is still in place.

What supports it

  • Sale proceeds are priced off the remaining lease term; the shorter the term at sale, the more the buyer discounts. www.sec.gov
  • Measure ULA can take 4.00% or 5.50% of gross consideration if the site is inside the City of Los Angeles and the price clears the threshold. finance.lacity.gov
  • System same-store sales fell 1.1% in Q3 FY2026 and the company guided to 50 to 60 closures in fiscal 2026. www.sec.gov
  • A § 1031 exchange is available for real property and can defer the gain if the owner wants to stay in real estate. www.irs.gov

What could break it

  • Sale certainty depends on a cap rate no government source publishes; the achievable price is unverifiable here. home.treasury.gov
  • Transfer tax, recapture and exchange deadlines make the execution path unforgiving. www.irs.gov
  • Selling crystallises the tax today rather than at an unknown later date. www.irs.gov

Option B — Renew with the incumbent tenant

Extend the existing operator and keep the income stream, at a rent and term the tenant will actually sign.

What supports it

  • Unemployment in the Los Angeles metro area fell to 4.4% in September 2026 from 5.5% a year earlier, and nonfarm payrolls rose 0.5%. www.bls.gov
  • Education and health services grew 3.8% year over year and carry 1,340.7 thousand jobs, the largest sector in the metro. www.bls.gov
  • The issuer's own filings describe a shrinking footprint and falling franchise rental revenue, which weakens the tenant's negotiating posture on rent. www.sec.gov
  • Median household income in Los Angeles city is $81,939 and in the county $90,112, supporting local consumer demand. www.census.gov

What could break it

  • A renewal that pushes the total remaining term, counting options, to 35 years or more creates a change in ownership and a reassessment under California law. www.boe.ca.gov
  • The tenant is closing 50 to 60 restaurants in fiscal 2026 and has discontinued its dividend and buyback; credit is deteriorating, not improving. www.sec.gov
  • Population in Los Angeles County is 3.2% below its 2020 estimates base, a demand headwind for marginal sites. www.census.gov
  • A renewal does not remove the concentration risk: one site, one operator, one intersection. www.sec.gov

Option C — Short extension, then sell

Buy time with a short, clean extension that preserves a marketable remaining term, then take the asset to market from a position of strength.

What supports it

  • A sale is worth more with a signed term certain than with a looming expiry, so a short extension can be value-accretive even at a modest rent. www.sec.gov
  • Keeping the total remaining term, including options, strictly under 35 years avoids the leasehold reassessment trigger. www.boe.ca.gov
  • The ten-year Treasury at 4.84% sets the risk-free anchor a buyer will use; the shorter the extension, the less duration risk the buyer takes. fred.stlouisfed.org

What could break it

  • The tenant may decline a short extension or demand a rent concession that offsets the benefit. www.sec.gov
  • Timing risk: if the market turns before the extension ends, the window closes. www.bls.gov
  • Two transaction events mean two sets of legal and advisory costs. finance.lacity.gov

Option D — Hold to expiry, then re-tenant or reposition

Run the lease out, then lease to another operator or redevelop the site.

What supports it

  • Fast-food sites are small-footprint, high-visibility parcels that can often be re-let to another limited-service or service-retail user. www.census.gov
  • Los Angeles city contains 8,304.2 people per square mile, among the densest urban footprints in the United States, which supports a range of alternative uses. www.census.gov
  • The county added 22,250 building permits in 2025, indicating continuing construction activity. www.census.gov

What could break it

  • Vacancy, tenant-improvement and leasing-commission costs are entirely the owner's during a dark period, and no official source can estimate them for this site. home.treasury.gov
  • The 60% thirty-year likelihood of an M≥6.7 earthquake in the Los Angeles region is an uninsured-balance-sheet risk that is worst when the site is vacant. pubs.usgs.gov
  • Re-tenanting a purpose-built restaurant box can require capital the owner may not want to commit at this stage. www.sec.gov
  • Los Angeles County population is 3.2% below its 2020 base, so re-leasing demand assumptions should not be optimistic. www.census.gov

Balancing wealth preservation against growth

Preservation and growth pull in different directions here, and the honest framing is that this asset is a preservation instrument exposed to a growth question. A net lease with a credit tenant preserves capital by producing a contractual, inflation-linked income stream; it grows only if the rent steps up or the cap rate falls. Right now the verified evidence says the rent is at risk — system same-store sales fell 1.1% in Q3 FY2026 — and the cap rate anchor has risen, with the ten-year Treasury at 4.84%. Both halves of the growth case are under pressure at the same time.

Preservation, meanwhile, is threatened by concentration rather than by income. One site and one operator in a region with a published 60% thirty-year likelihood of a magnitude 6.7 or greater earthquake is an undiversified position regardless of how good the lease looks. If this property is a meaningful share of your net worth, the diversification argument is stronger than any argument about the lease.

This is reasoning from published evidence, not personalised investment advice. Your tax position, liquidity needs, other holdings and time horizon all matter and none of them is known here.
