ASSET A · NET LEASE RESEARCH● Evidence snapshot · 2026-11-08

Executive summary

Written for the owner of one anonymised single-tenant net-leased Jack in the Box property in the Los Angeles area, with a lease expiry reported somewhere between 2030 and 2035.

The short answer

Prepare to exit, but do not sign anything irreversible until four documents are in hand. The verified evidence points one way: the operator's system is contracting, its rental income is falling, and its cost floor is fixed by law at $20.00 an hour. The local economy is stable but not growing into new supply. That combination rewards an owner who controls the timing of the exit over an owner who waits to be told the terms.

What it does not justify is a decision made today. The rent, the expiry date, the obligor and the tax basis are unknown. Any ranking of sell versus renew built on those blanks would be a guess dressed as arithmetic, so this dashboard refuses to produce one.

3. The risk balance

Risks that argue for exiting

  • Tenant credit is deteriorating while the system contracts. www.sec.gov
  • Concentration: one site, one operator, one intersection, no internal diversification. www.sec.gov
  • Earthquake likelihood of 60% over thirty years, generally uninsured. pubs.usgs.gov
  • Cap-rate repricing risk while the ten-year Treasury sits near 4.84%. fred.stlouisfed.org
  • Transfer-tax exposure has a known, dated schedule and will not get cheaper by waiting. finance.lacity.gov

Risks that argue for patience

  • Selling crystallises tax now; a § 1031 exchange can defer it but must be executed to deadline. www.irs.gov
  • No cap rate can be verified from an official source, so the achievable price is unknown here. home.treasury.gov
  • The local labour market is genuinely improving: unemployment 4.4%, payrolls up 0.5%. www.bls.gov
  • A sale with a short remaining term is discounted by buyers; an extension can restore term. www.sec.gov
  • Median household income of $81,939 in the city and $90,112 in the county supports local spend. www.census.gov

What this page deliberately does not give you

A price. A cap rate. A probability that the tenant renews. A ranking that says sell beats renew by a stated margin. None of those can be derived from an official source for this property, and inventing them would be the single most damaging thing this report could do. The four documents in step 1 supply them; until then the honest answer is that the decision is not yet determinable.
