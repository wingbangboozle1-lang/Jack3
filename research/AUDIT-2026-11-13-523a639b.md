### Model-vintage finding

The UCERF3 probabilities are source-confirmed, but their stated 30-year period starts in 2014. The USGS's current National Seismic Hazard Model page lists a 2023 CONUS model and a revised 2023 model released in 2026; USGS describes that model as a time-independent probabilistic **ground-shaking** model. It is a different measure from the UCERF3 California rupture-probability table, so the numbers must not be swapped or combined. The current site already labels its UCERF3 rows “from 2014”; a future improvement is to add an explicit method note and, if an appropriate official regional/parcel metric is directly extracted, a separately labeled NSHM row. No new probability or property risk is inferred here.

Official references: [USGS National Seismic Hazard Model overview](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/national-seismic-hazard-model) · [USGS 2023 50-State Long-Term NSHM, revised model documentation](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/2023-50-state-long-term-national-seismic-hazard-model-0).
