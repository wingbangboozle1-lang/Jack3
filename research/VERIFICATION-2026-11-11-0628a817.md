# Independent verification and integration audit — 2026-11-11 (session 0628a817)

## Scope and limitations

This is a dated audit of the canonical register at main commit `c90b345`; later
integrations can change the row numbers and resolutions. It is not a claim that
an official source establishes a value, likelihood, or suitability for the
unidentified property. Asset A's location, lease, credit, rent, expenses, debt,
basis, insurance, owner circumstances, and offered prices are not known. No
private facts were requested, collected, written or published. The evidence
below comprises public regional statistics, agency rules, forecasts and
issuer statements only.

The per-row ledger records the inherited status, linked publisher, and our
review of the observed *row or its evidence boundary*. A **review** entry is a
material qualification or necessary correction, not an acceptable verified
fact about the asset. A **blank** means no asset value is established, even if
an agency link provides a route to investigate privately. Analysis, forecasts
and derived arithmetic are not observations. Search indexing was needed for
the client-rendered 2025 Dashboard detail and later pages of the SCAG PDF;
the links in the ledger are the official publisher, not the search engine.
The current public register was read before concurrent fixes landed; this file
retains the audit history and does not imply the same defects remain afterward.

## Priority findings and resolutions required

1. **Census dates and method.** City 3,869,089 and county 9,694,934 are July 1,
   **2025** V2025 estimates, not 2026. Their percent changes are measured from
   an April 2020 estimates base. The city poverty figure is the sub-county ACS
   five-year series, not county SAIPE; the QuickFacts methodology explicitly
   distinguishes them: [Census city](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/losangelescitycalifornia),
   [county](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/losangelescountycalifornia),
   [poverty definitions](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/note/IPE120225).
2. **Graduation definitions.** The 2025 district overview is 86.5% and the
   2026 overview 87.6%; since 2022 the Dashboard indicator combines four- and
   five-year cohorts. Its 2025 English-learner breakdown calls 64.1% the four-year
   component of the combined indicator, not necessarily a standalone four-year
   ACGR. The 2026 [DataQuest report](https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/dqcensus/CohRate.aspx?agglevel=district&year=2025-26&cds=1964733)
   separately gives **87.4%** four-year adjusted cohort graduation, 24,857 of
   28,447, under its default **Non-Charter** filter. It is not the same
   population or indicator as the Dashboard. We did not reproduce the older
   asserted 86.2% component, which must remain blank if retained. The 2019
   Dashboard also included DASS one-year results; don't treat the entire
   multi-year series as a consistent methodology. [2025 overview](https://www.caschooldashboard.org/reports/19647330000000/2025),
   [2026 overview](https://www.caschooldashboard.org/reports/19647330000000/2026),
   [CDE methodology](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/cm/graddatafiles.asp).
3. **Property tax and transfer tax.** A below-market assessed base, particular
   exemption or reassessment outcome is not established. The 2% limit concerns
   the factored base-year value and has exceptions, including decline-in-value
   restoration and assessable changes. For leasehold transfers, the original and
   remaining 35-year tests differ; termination by merger can be excepted. City
   base and ULA taxes may use different tax bases; the base is assessed per $500
   **or fraction**. ULA has a 4% and a
   5.5% tier, exemptions and annually adjusted thresholds. This asset's actual
   jurisdiction and tax amounts are unknown. [BOE](https://boe.ca.gov/pdf/pub800-10.pdf),
   [City ULA](https://finance.lacity.gov/faq/measure-ula),
   [County transfer tax](https://www.lavote.gov/home/recorder/property-document-recording/documentary-transfer-taxes/general-info).
4. **Earthquake dates and provenance.** UCERF3's 30-year probabilities are from
   **2014 to 2044**, not a newly estimated 2026–2056 probability. They are
   regional earthquake probabilities, not loss probabilities. A different USGS
   source provides the 2008 ShakeOut scenario. The two CAL FIRE incident links
   must both accompany the combined 6,845 + 9,419 = 16,264 arithmetic.
   [UCERF3](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2015/3009/pdf/fs2015-3009.pdf),
   [ShakeOut](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1150/),
   [Palisades](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/palisades-fire),
   [Eaton](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/eaton-fire).
5. **Forecast precision.** SCAG prints 0.49% compounded annual regional
   employment growth for 2022–2050. A separate 0.50% calculation on its rounded
   endpoints must be explicitly identified as derived, not quoted as its exact
   reported number. SCAG's adopted 2024 planning projections are conditional,
   not a parcel forecast and not directly comparable with subsequent Census
   vintage estimates. The official 2030/2035 county projections are available;
   the earlier site contained them and they should not silently disappear.
   [SCAG forecast](https://www.scag.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/23-2987-tr-demographics-growth-forecast-final-040424.pdf),
   [SCAG 2035 table](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2024-05/twg021524agn.pdf),
   [CA DOF K–12 2026 series](https://dof.ca.gov/forecasting/demographics/public-k-12-graded-enrollment/).
6. **Sources are not conclusions.** Split or cite both sources for composite
   evidence (city plus county Census; BLS plus FRED; USGS ShakeOut plus both fire
   agencies). A cited issuer document is context, not proof of this lease or
   the best decision. Qualitative recommendations and severity labels must be
   visibly identified as judgment; neither a required ten-year extension nor
   the claim that marketing is universally lowest-risk follows from the data.
7. **Rates and timing.** FRED shows 4.88% for 2026-11-09 as of this audit;
   4.86% on 2026-11-06 remains correct when dated, but is not the latest. A
   Treasury yield is a market reference, not a mandatory discount-rate floor;
   historical CPI and ECI prints do not mathematically bound future inflation.
   [FRED DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10).
8. **Historical model limitation.** The repository-only `scripts/projection.py`
   hard-codes ten years, describes it as ending in 2035, applies the exit cap to
   normalized contractual rent instead of documented NOI, and deducts a void
   from rent around the terminal date despite describing it as after expiry.
   It also excludes tax, costs and debt. Its numerical output is not included in
   the current canonical Pages site. Do not promote it without fixing timing,
   a consistent cash-flow basis and tests. `scripts/decision_engine.py` is a
   separate fail-closed comparison: only supplied dated after-tax equity flows,
   a supplied terminal cash flow and a supplied required return; it never
   collects, persists or invents a property number.
9. **Publication integration.** PR #47 introduced two root Markdown files that
   made the actual Jekyll inventory gate fail. PR #42's later wildcard Markdown
   exclusion prevents them being published, but its 20-page site leaves the
   earlier session's 12-page `test_publication.py` assumptions stale. PR #50
   repaired the inventory; its unknown-value test still points to the historical
   evidence register, and PR #51 proposes a canonical-register test. Both owners
   were notified. Never disable the regression gate to make it pass.
10. **Unavailable map tools.** The FEMA Map Service Center and National Risk
    Index returned server errors via the research fetcher; the official [FEMA
    flood-maps page](https://www.fema.gov/flood-maps) confirms the service. No
    parcel flood zone, risk-index score or insurability is assigned.
