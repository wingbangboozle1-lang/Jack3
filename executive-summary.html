Executive summary

The decision, the evidence behind it, the risks of every route, and the limits of what public data can establish.

BASE CASE · CONDITIONAL RECOMMENDATION

Run both tracks in parallel; sell before expiry unless a long, credit-supported extension is signed.

Start a confidential sale process 24–36 months before the earliest contractual expiry while requiring the tenant to state renewal terms in writing. Sell if a documented after-tax offer is acceptable; extend only against an enforceable, credit-supported extension of at least ten years at market rent. This is a rule for preserving optionality and wealth, not a valuation.

Risk balance for wealth preservation

Manageable risk, not zero risk

Concentration is the dominant risk

A single-tenant net lease looks safe until the term ends. Holding to expiry concentrates tenant credit, lease-rollover, rate and catastrophe risk into one short window. Selling early spreads the decision across several years and converts an illiquid single asset into a choice.

The cost of acting early is real: transfer taxes, a possible 4% ULA surcharge, capital-gain tax and reinvestment risk. Those are computable from published rules once the owner's figures exist.

Why not simply renew

Renewal is attractive only if it is long, at market rent, and backed by a verified obligor. The issuer's chain-wide results cannot supply that. A short or below-market extension locks in the weakest part of the hold case and removes the ability to sell into a good bid.

Black-swan exposure is not diversified by renewing: the same parcel keeps the same earthquake, wildfire and flood exposure. Insurance verification and a concentration cap on net worth are required under either route.
