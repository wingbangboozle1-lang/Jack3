R-01 Tenant does not renew and vacates at expiry, leaving the site dark
Brand is contracting: 86 closures vs 31 openings in FY2025; guidance is 2,050–2,100 restaurants by FY2026 year end versus 2,136 at FY2025; same-store sales -4.2% for FY2025, -7.4% in Q4.		 S21 S22 Medium-high High Obtain the franchisee's unit-level P&L and the franchisor's renewal intent in writing before expiry; negotiate an early extension with a rent reset while the site is still trading; keep 12–18 months of carry reserve to fund downtime. Hold Renew
R-02 Franchisee insolvency before expiry (lease rejection in bankruptcy)
The lease counterparty may be a franchisee entity rather than the corporate parent; franchisee financial statements are not public.		 S21 Low-medium High Confirm guarantor structure and any corporate guarantee; require personal or entity guaranty on renewal; monitor franchisee unit count and payment record. Hold Renew
R-03 Rent escalation clauses lag inflation, eroding real income
Los Angeles CPI-U is running at 3.7% year over year (September 2026). Many legacy QSR leases escalate at 1.5–2% or 10% every five years.		 S01 High Medium At renewal, index escalations to CPI or set 3% fixed; model the real (inflation-adjusted) rent, not the nominal rent. Hold Renew Sell
R-04 Fast-food labour cost keeps rising, squeezing tenant rent coverage
California fast food minimum wage is $20.00/hour (from April 1, 2024); state minimum wage is $16.90 (2026); City of Los Angeles minimum wage is $18.42 (from July 1, 2026). The Fast Food Council can adjust the $20.00 rate.		 S11 S12 S13 Medium Medium-high Underwrite rent coverage at a stressed unit-level margin; require the tenant to disclose unit EBITDA at renewal; avoid percentage-rent-only structures. Hold Renew Sell
R-05 Cap rates widen as financing costs exceed net lease yields
Overall single-tenant net lease cap rates rose 10 bp to 6.92% in Q3 2026, the highest in over a decade; the Boulder Group reports permanent financing costs now generally exceed net lease cap rates. The 10-year Treasury was 4.90% on 4 November 2026; the Fed raised its target range to 3.75%–4.00% in September 2026.		 S24 S04 Medium-high High If selling, sell into a tight market rather than a widening one; keep the option to hold, which is not marked to market; avoid short-fuse financing on a hold. Sell Hold
R-06 Exit taxes and fees consume 4–10% of price
Base transfer tax in the City of Los Angeles is $5.60 per $1,000 (0.56%). Measure ULA adds 4% on sales above $5,400,000 and 5.5% above $10,900,000 (flagged, see F-01). Brokerage and closing costs are additional.		 S14 S28 Certain on sale High Price the all-in exit cost into the sell/hold comparison (see Scenarios); consider timing sales just after the 1 July ULA threshold indexation; confirm whether the value sits below the $5.4M cliff. Sell
R-07 Sale triggers depreciation recapture and capital gains tax
Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain is taxed at up to 25%; long-term capital gain on real property at up to 20%; net investment income tax may also apply. Section 1031 allows deferral only if a replacement is identified within 45 days and closed within 180 days.		 S16 S15 Certain on sale High If selling, run a 1031 exchange with a qualified intermediary lined up before listing; otherwise assume a 15–25% haircut to net proceeds. Confirm basis with a CPA first (F-07). Sell
R-08 Buyer discounts price for reassessment on sale
Proposition 13 limits annual assessed-value growth to 2% for a held property, but a sale reassesses to market. County-wide roll value rose 4.42% in 2026 while most parcels were capped at 2%.		 S05 High Medium Recognise that holding preserves a low tax basis — a real, quantifiable advantage that is lost on sale and only partly recovered by a buyer's price adjustment. Sell Hold
R-09 Insurance cost and availability deteriorate after the 2025 fires
The January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires destroyed 9,414 and 6,837 structures respectively and caused an estimated $5.2–10.1 billion of lost economic output; the Insurance Commissioner imposed a one-year moratorium on non-renewals and cancellations in affected areas.		 S19 S20 Medium Medium Confirm the premium pass-through mechanics in the lease; obtain a current quote before any renewal term is agreed; retain a structural reserve for premium spikes. Hold Renew Sell

R-11 Population decline and weak office-adjacent daytime demand
Los Angeles County's population fell 3.2% between April 2020 and July 2025 (10,014,009 to 9,694,934). Metro information-sector employment is down 5.7% year over year and professional/business services down 0.5%.		 S02 S01 Medium Medium Weight the trade area toward residential and institutional anchors (health, education, tourism) rather than office; education and health employment is the strongest sector at +3.8%. B-01 Major southern San Andreas or blind-thrust earthquake
USGS models a M7.8 southern San Andreas event at about 1,800 deaths and $213 billion of economic losses (2008 dollars), of which $112.7 billion is property damage and $96.2 billion business interruption.		 S07 Low in any year Severe Commission a seismic evaluation and retrofit estimate now; carry earthquake cover or accept and document the uninsured exposure; keep the building's non-structural elements braced. January 2025 showed that wildfire is no longer a wildland-only risk in Los Angeles County: 9,414 structures were destroyed in the Eaton Fire and 6,837 in the Palisades Fire, with roughly 6,800 businesses and 47,000 workers affected. The post-fire environment produced a one-year moratorium on non-renewals and cancellations in affected areas; the CRS reports heavy reliance on the FAIR Plan in the Palisades before the fires. Risk by decision

Selling now removes…

  • Tenant non-renewal risk (R-01), franchisee insolvency (R-02) and brand restructuring (B-09).
  • Seismic and wildfire exposure on the building (B-01, B-02) — though not on the sale price if the buyer prices it in.
  • Illiquidity and concentration.

…and creates

  • Certain tax and friction: 4.5% fees, plus 25% recapture and up to 20% capital gains, plus state tax (R-06, R-07).
  • Reinvestment risk: the proceeds must be redeployed at 6.87%-type franchisee-QSR yields while financing costs exceed cap rates (R-05).
  • Loss of the low Proposition 13 basis and of the redevelopment option.

Holding creates…

  • Concentration in one parcel, one tenant and one submarket.
  • Exposure to a dark store at expiry and to the capital calls that follow it.
  • Exposure to a cap-rate widening that a buyer would otherwise have absorbed.

…and preserves

  • The low tax basis, the deferral option, and the land/redevelopment upside.
  • The ability to sell after an extension, when the asset prices tighter (R-13).

The uninsured tail, stated plainly

A magnitude-7.8 southern San Andreas event is modelled by the USGS at roughly 1,800 deaths and $213 billion of economic losses, of which about $112.7 billion is property damage and $96.2 billion is business interruption (S07). That is a regional model from 2008 in 2008 dollars, not a forecast for [PROPERTY-A]. It is used here for one purpose: to size the tail that insurance either covers or does not. Earthquake cover for unreinforced or pre-1976 construction is expensive and often sub-limited; the owner should know, in writing, exactly what is excluded before assuming the tail is transferred.

The January 2025 fires show the same logic for wildfire: 9,414 structures destroyed in Eaton and 6,837 in Palisades, about 6,800 businesses and 47,000 workers affected, and an insurance market that responded with a one-year moratorium on non-renewals (S19 S20). Wildfire is no longer a foothill-only problem in this county.
