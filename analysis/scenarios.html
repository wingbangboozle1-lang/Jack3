Projection and scenario model

A deterministic, fully disclosed model. It does not know the rent or the NOI — so it is built to be scale-free in percentage terms and to accept the real number the moment the owner supplies it.

The rent, NOI, basis and lease terms are private and are the subject of flags F-06 and F-07. What the model does provide is the structure of the decision: which variables dominate, and how far a variable has to move before the answer changes.

Option A — hold to expiry versus Option B — sell now and exchange

Both options are run unlevered and pre-tax over the same 7-year horizon, so they are directly comparable. Option B assumes the proceeds are redeployed through a Section 1031 exchange into a diversified net-lease position at 6.87% — which is the only way selling avoids an immediate tax bill.

The finding. Holding wins in every income band. The margin is about 0.8 percentage points a year below the ULA threshold and widens to about 1.6 points above it, because a sale at those values pays the ULA surtax twice — once on the way out and again, economically, when the replacement asset is later sold. Holding only loses if the exit cap widens beyond roughly 7.2%–7.9%, which would be a severe repricing from today's 6.5% entry level.

At a 6.50% entry, a 75 bp widening at exit still leaves a 7.2% IRR — because the income return and rent growth carry it. That is why the hold case is robust to all but a severe repricing.

What this means in practice. At a 35% basis, a sale at $5.4 million leaves roughly 24% of the gain with the tax authorities. A 1031 exchange defers that, but only if a replacement is identified within 45 days — so the exchange must be arranged before Listing, not after closing.

Limits of the model

  • No leverage. Debt would amplify both the income return and the cap-rate risk, and financing costs currently exceed net-lease cap rates (S24), so negative leverage would lower returns.
  • No vacancy scenario. The model assumes the lease runs to expiry. The vacancy case is handled qualitatively on the Risk page because its cost depends on the tenant improvements a replacement user needs — unknown.
  • No redevelopment value. The land may be worth more as housing or mixed use than as a restaurant. That upside is described on the Decision page and excluded here because zoning capacity is unverified (F-09).
  • Single path, no Monte Carlo. A probability distribution over cap rates and rents would imply a precision the underlying inputs do not support.
