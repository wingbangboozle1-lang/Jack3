F-01 · Measure ULA rate table Medium — affects sale proceeds and the timing of any exit

Measure ULA rates of 4% (sales above $5,400,000 and below $10,900,000) and 5.5% ($10,900,000 and above) for closings after 30 June 2026 are carried as FLAGGED, not verified. The City of Los Angeles Office of Finance page could not be loaded during this session (HTTP 404 on the deep link that was tried). The rates were corroborated by three independent secondary sources that each cite the Office of Finance, and by the County Recorder's published figures, but no official page was read directly.

What is needed to close it: Read the current rate table on finance.lacity.gov (Measure ULA page) or obtain a written rate confirmation from the Office of Finance for the closing date.

F-02 · Repeal / reform status of Measure ULA Medium — a repeal would remove a 4–5.5% exit cost

Secondary reports conflict. Some state the statewide transfer-tax-cap initiative was withdrawn from the November 2026 ballot and that no repeal path exists in 2026; others still describe it as a live ballot measure. What IS verified from the Secretary of State's certified list is that no transfer-tax-cap measure appears among the 15 propositions on the November 3, 2026 ballot. The verified conclusion is therefore limited to: no relief is on the 2026 ballot. Future repeal or reform is unverified.

What is needed to close it: Re-check the Secretary of State certified list and the City of Los Angeles after the November 3, 2026 election; monitor any 2027–2028 initiatives.

Related sources: S17 S27

F-03 · Parcel-level seismic classification High — drives retrofit cost and insurability

No parcel-level Alquist-Priolo fault-zone or liquefaction-zone determination is made anywhere in this report. The property address is withheld for privacy, and the California Geological Survey regulatory maps must be queried by parcel (CGS EQ Zapp / City of Los Angeles ZIMAS). Central Los Angeles contains mapped fault and liquefaction zones, but asserting a specific classification for [PROPERTY-A] would be an inference, not a verified fact.

What is needed to close it: Parcel-level CGS seismic-hazard report and ZIMAS printout, obtained under NDA by the owner or counsel.

Related sources: S07

F-04 · Parcel-level fire hazard severity zone Medium — affects insurance availability and pricing

CAL FIRE states that Fire Hazard Severity Zones in Local Responsibility Areas are determined with the local jurisdiction, and that parcel-level confirmation comes from that jurisdiction. Central Los Angeles is inside the City of Los Angeles (an LRA), so no FHSZ class is asserted for [PROPERTY-A].

What is needed to close it: City of Los Angeles FHSZ lookup for the parcel, plus the insurer's own wildfire scoring.

Related sources: S08

F-05 · Parcel-level flood zone Medium — affects insurance and lender requirements

No FEMA flood-zone determination is asserted. FEMA map service queries require the address or coordinates, both withheld here. Portions of central Los Angeles near the Los Angeles River are mapped; that is area context, not a parcel finding.

What is needed to close it: FEMA Map Service Center flood-zone determination for the parcel; obtain an elevation certificate if in or near a Special Flood Hazard Area.

F-06 · Actual rent, NOI and rent coverage High — the single largest driver of the decision

The lease rent, escalation schedule, CAM/tax/insurance pass-throughs, percentage-rent clause, renewal options, tenant improvement obligations and the franchisee's unit-level EBITDA are all private. Every financial output on this site is therefore a model driven by disclosed assumptions, not a valuation of [PROPERTY-A]. No NOI figure is asserted as fact.

What is needed to close it: Lease abstract, rent roll, three years of CAM reconciliations, franchisee unit P&L and the franchisor's unit-level AUV for the trade area.

F-08 · Tenant identity and franchisee credit High — corporate vs franchisee paper can move cap rate by 50–100 bp

Whether the lease is with Jack in the Box Inc. (corporate guarantee) or with a franchisee entity determines credit quality and the applicable cap rate. Franchisee financial statements are not public. The corporate parent's own unit count is declining and same-store sales are negative, which is disclosed; the franchisee's condition is unknown.

What is needed to close it: Lease guarantor pages, franchisee financial statements, and the franchisor's most recent Franchise Disclosure Document (Item 19 and Item 20 tables).

F-10 · Fatality counts for the January 2025 fires Low — no effect on the property decision; disclosed for completeness

The County of Los Angeles anniversary fact sheet lists 19 confirmed fatalities for the Eaton Fire and 12 for the Palisades Fire. Contemporaneous reporting from February 2025 reported 17 Eaton fatalities as of that date. Counts were revised during recovery. Both numbers are recorded here; the County fact sheet figure is used in the dataset and the discrepancy is disclosed rather than resolved.

What is needed to close it: Final LA County Medical Examiner determination.
