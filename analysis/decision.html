Recommendation

Primary recommendation: Option B, executed as a conditional hold with prepared optionality — not a passive hold.

  1. Do not list in 2026–2027. The model shows a 0.8–1.6 percentage-point annual return advantage to holding, and the market is at a decade-high cap rate with financing costs above yields (S24): a seller is competing for a thin pool of all-cash buyers. Selling also triggers 4.5% of friction and, above $5.4 million, a 4%–5.5% ULA surtax (S14 S28).
  2. Convert the lease into an asset before the market tests it. Open an extension conversation now, while the unit is trading. A five-year extension at a market rent reset converts a five-year lease into a ten-year lease and lifts the achievable price by more than the rent concession typically costs.
  3. Buy information, not opinions. Commission an appraisal, a property-condition report, a Phase I environmental site assessment and a seismic evaluation this year. Each is cheap relative to the decision and each closes a flag.
  4. Test the redevelopment option in parallel. A zoning and entitlement feasibility study tells the owner whether the land is worth more than the income stream. If it is, the expiry date stops being a risk and becomes the trigger for Option C.
  5. Hold the sell option live. If any trigger below fires, sell — and sell through a 1031 exchange arranged in advance, not in haste.

Why this is the best answer for [OWNER-1] as an individual, not just for the asset: it keeps the low Proposition 13 basis, defers an avoidable tax bill, and preserves every exit route while adding the one thing the asset currently lacks — information. It accepts manageable risk (tenant, seismic, rate) and avoids the two unmanageable ones (a forced sale into a soft bid, and an unplanned tax event).

What would change the answer

Toward selling sooner

  • The tenant signals closure, or unit-level sales are materially below the system average.
  • The appraisal lands just above $5.4 million, making the ULA cliff decisive — restructure the price or wait for the next 1 July threshold indexation.
  • A rate-driven cap-rate compression makes the income stream expensive relative to its risk.
  • The owner needs liquidity, or the estate plan requires a step-up.

Toward holding longer

  • The tenant extends: a ten-year lease re-rates the asset and makes a later sale cheaper in cap-rate terms.
  • Entitlements are secured: the land option dominates the income option.
  • Rates fall and the income stream is re-rated upward.
  • The 2028 Games deliver the anticipated demand lift to the trade area.
