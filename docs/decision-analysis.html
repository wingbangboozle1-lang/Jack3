Option-by-option detail

Option A — Sell before expiry (2026–2028 window)

  • Pros: captures remaining-term premium; eliminates tenant-credit tail risk; liquidity unlocked; potential 1031 deferral into diversified income assets/DSTs; Olympics-window demand (2028) could lift sale pricing for well-located urban assets.
  • Cons: crystallizes capital gains + depreciation recapture (federal + CA); forfeits renewal rent bump (often the single largest value creator for NNN QSR); exits a replacement for basis that Prop 13 preserves; redeploys into a higher-rate world (10Y 4.90%).
  • Best if: any sell-trigger from the executive summary fires, or a bid ≥ +15% over independent value opinion appears.

Option B — Renew at expiry and hold (2030–2035 → 2040+)

  • Pros: long-term NNN passive income; tenant pays opex/taxes/insurance (inflation hedge); Prop 13 basis protection; renewal reset typically re-marks rent to market; preserves 30+ year wealth-building vehicle (QSR real estate with drive-through is scarce urban land).
  • Cons: single-asset concentration; term decay risk; tenant credit (JACK stock −72% FY2020→FY2025; SSS negative; activist campaign; restructuring); LA County population projected to decline → flat demand growth; illiquid exit if needed mid-term.
  • Best if: renewal is achieved with a meaningful reset and re-affirmed NNN + corporate/franchisee guarantee; you need the income, not the lump sum.

Option C — Hybrid: negotiate now, keep optionality (recommended)

  • Mechanics: (1) estoppel certificate within 30 days; (2) open extension talks 24–36 months before expiry; (3) target rent reset +10–20% (illustrative), NNN re-affirmation, updated guarantee + SNDA, % rent if applicable, notice deadlines calendared; (4) simultaneously: broker opinion of value ×2, sale-ready package (rent roll, lease abstract, insurance certificates, permits), 1031 pre-planning with CPA; (5) sell only on trigger breach.
  • Pros: highest expected value; keeps Olympic-window sale available later; de-risks the expiry cliff; tests tenant commitment early at zero cost.
  • Cons: requires active management; possible negotiation friction with tenant; broker opinions cost ~0.1–0.3% (fee estimate — not verified; typical practice).

Explicit framing: neither option is demonstrably superior — decide early; do not drift to expiry.
  • Evidence dashboard (original) — the first structured evidence board built in this repo (per-row source links, scripts/build.py + scripts/validate.py, data/evidence.json).

This page’s Option C (hybrid: negotiate now, keep optionality) is the decision framework that operationalizes the convergence shared by both framings: act on the owner’s timeline, not the tenant’s; keep the LA28-window sale fully available; renew only on hard conditions (corporate guarantee + escalations + performance). Where the companion study’s default differs (sell-first vs negotiate-first), the difference is framing, not data — every factual input is the same official series, and both agree the worst outcome is drifting to 2030–2035 expiry without a decision.

Tax note (general information, not tax advice)

🧾
A sale triggers federal capital gains + depreciation recapture (gain on depreciable property generally taxed up to 25% federal on recapture) and California tax on the gain; ongoing NNN income is taxed as ordinary passive income with depreciation shelter. A §1031 exchange can defer all federal and California gain only into like-kind property identified/acquired on strict deadlines. Engage a qualified CPA/tax attorney before signing anything. No parcel-specific tax bill or basis data appears here by design.
