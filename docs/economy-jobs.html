What the numbers mean

  • Labour market: LA County SA unemployment 4.8% (Sep 2026, EDD — vs 5.0% Aug revised, 5.6% a year earlier; below CA 5.1% SA, above US 4.2% SA); LA MSA UE 4.4% (FRED, Sep 2026, NSA). County nonfarm +20,300 / +0.4% YoY (NSA); LA MSA nonfarm +0.6% YoY (BLS, Aug 2026, NSA). Payrolls flat at ~6.30M — no recession signal, no boom either. Methodologies and geographies differ — see Flagged for Review (F-17).
  • Restaurant pipeline: LA County accommodation & food services +8,600 YoY and restaurants & other eating places +7,300 YoY (EDD, Sep 2026, NSA) — your direct comps are adding staff; leisure & hospitality +8,900. Educational & health services +37,500 YoY (health care ≈ 83% — see F-18).
  • Inflation vs escalators: LA CPI-U +3.7% 12-month (Sep 2026, NSA — core +2.6%, energy +20.3%, shelter +3.7%; BLS). Federal funds 3.75–4.00% (FOMC hold Oct 28, 2026, 10–2). Typical stacked 5-yr bumps (~1.55%/yr) lose real purchasing power to LA inflation — the renewal reset matters more than contractual escalators.
  • Fast-food wage floor: AB 1228 — $20.00/hr effective 2024-04-01 (DIR-confirmed); Fast Food Council may adjust annually (statutory cap: lesser of 3.5% or CPI — statute; verify current rate at dir.ca.gov/AB1228 ↗). Tenant labor cost is structurally higher; pass-through into same-store sales is partial.
  • Consumer scale: $44.0B food & accommodation sales countywide (2022) and $81.35B retail sales in the city (2022, $21,281 per capita). The operating pond for a QSR is enormous; capture rate, not market size, is the question.
  • Chain-area jobs base: 3.99M county payroll jobs across 304,988 employer establishments (2023), +2.3% employment growth 2022→2023 — modest but positive.
  • Commute burden: 30.4–30.7 minutes average — heavy car dependence favors drive-through; weekday lunch/dinner dayparts near employment nodes.
⚠️
Watch item. LA metro payrolls are essentially flat (6,311K Jun → 6,300K Sep 2026) while California unemployment sits a full point above the national rate. A flat-to-soft 2026–27 is a headwind for same-store sales — exactly what JACK reported (system SSS −1.1% Q3 FY2026). Underwrite the renewal on contractual escalators, not on sales growth.
