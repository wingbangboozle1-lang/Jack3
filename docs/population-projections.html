Reading the projection

  • 2030: 9,727,305 (−1.3% vs 2025) — essentially flat through the early expiry window.
  • 2035: 9,708,647 (−1.5%) — flat-to-negative when the current lease expires.
  • 2040: 9,637,524 (−2.2%); 2050: 9,390,823 (−4.7%); 2060: 8,961,332 (−9.1%); 2070: 8,413,331 (−14.6%).
  • Implied 2025→2035 CAGR ≈ −0.15%/yr; 2025→2060 ≈ −0.27%/yr. California as a whole keeps growing (39.5M → 41.1M peak ~2049, then slowly declining) — LA bleeds relative share to inland counties.
📉
Investment implication. A shrinking county does not kill a dense infill drive-through QSR — it caps growth. Do not underwrite any revenue growth into the renewal negotiation; underwrite rent escalations and the renewal reset. Note the 2020 Census (10.014M) vs DOF 2024-baseline 2020 figure (10.012M) — series differ by methodology; QuickFacts/2020 Census used for historical fact, DOF P-2A for projections only (see Flagged for Review).

Why LA shrinks in the projection

DOF methodology ages the population forward with fertility, mortality and migration assumptions; LA shows decades of negative net domestic migration and declining births. SCAG (the regional government) is more optimistic than DOF, but both agree on flat to modestly negative growth — neither forecaster shows a boom. Any pro-forma assuming >0.5%/yr population growth for LA County should be treated as aggressive.
