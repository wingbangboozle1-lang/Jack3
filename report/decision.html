The decision

Sell. Extend first only if the tenant commits. Then exit.

Six strategies ranked by their worst case, a risk-balanced reading against both stated objectives, the tax mechanics, and the triggers that should reopen the decision.

The decision

Sell. If the tenant will commit in writing to a long extension now, extend first and then sell. Do not renew at expiry and do not assume the option is yours to exercise.

Balancing preservation against growth

The brief asks for both wealth preservation and opportunity for growth, at a manageable level of risk. Those objectives pull in different directions, and the model quantifies the trade rather than hiding it.

Preservation first

S1 — sell now

Highest floor of any strategy (11.62 R) and the narrowest spread. Resolves the concentration problem: one illiquid, single-tenant, single-county position becomes diversifiable capital. That benefit is independent of any view on cap rates.

Balanced

S5 — extend, then sell

Best mean rank (6th / 1st / 2nd across the scenarios). Captures the extension premium while still exiting. The condition is demanding: a tenant that is publicly closing restaurants must agree to commit to a longer term.

Growth

S4 or S6 — hold and re-let or redevelop

Highest expected value (S4) or the most robust hold case (S6), both with materially lower floors. Appropriate only if the owner can absorb the downside without distress and has independently confirmed demand or entitlement.

The trap in the 'growth' column

Both growth strategies require evidence that does not yet exist: confirmed non-Jack demand for the box, or a zoning position that supports redevelopment. Without one of those, a hold is not a growth strategy — it is a bet that a contracting tenant stays, which the model already prices as the worst branch.

What not to do

  • Do not spend money on a renewal negotiation before establishing whether the site is in the closure programme.
  • Do not price the asset off a single broker cap rate. No official cap rate exists.
  • Do not assume the renewal option is yours. The issuer discloses its options as generally at the company's sole discretion.
  • Do not list without a §1031 plan if reinvestment is intended; the 45-day identification clock starts at closing.
  • Do not treat brand-level same-store sales as this restaurant's performance, in either direction.
