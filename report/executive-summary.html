Overview

The decision, the evidence behind it, and the conditions

Six findings, one recommendation, and the specific facts that would overturn it.

The answer in one paragraph

Sell. A 2030-2035 expiry is not a date the owner controls: the tenant's own leases are disclosed as renewable at the company's sole discretion, and the company is running a disclosed 150-200 unit closure programme timed to franchise-agreement termination dates. Selling now converts a single-tenant, single-county, one-counterparty position into diversifiable capital and removes cap-rate risk. If, and only if, the tenant will commit in writing to a long extension now, extend first and then sell the lengthened lease — it has the better base case and the best rank stability. Do not plan the decision around renewing at expiry.

Six findings that decide it

1. The tenant is contracting, and its own closure clock runs to the expiry date

Jack in the Box Inc. disclosed a 150-200 restaurant block-closure programme. The targeted restaurants are largely ones "in the system for over three decades", and the remaining closures occur "based upon respective franchise agreement termination dates". The system shrank from 2,136 restaurants at FY2025 year end, with 86 closures against 31 openings during FY2025, and stood at 2,115 restaurants by 2026-07-05.

Sources: Jack in the Box Inc., Q4 and full-year 2025 earnings release (Exhibit 99.1); Jack in the Box Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended 2025-09-28; Jack in the Box Inc., Form 10-Q for the period ended 2026-07-05.

3. The county is losing population, and official forecasters disagree on the sign

Measured Census estimates show Los Angeles County at 9,694,934 persons on 1 July 2025, -3.2% against the 2020 estimates base, and the city at 3,869,089 persons (-0.8%). Against that, SCAG's most recent adopted regional plan projected growth, while the California Department of Finance projects decline. (The projection workbooks themselves are unread — F-04.) Two official bodies disagree on the direction of travel for the same county.

This file underwrites to the measurement and treats growth as upside. A drive-thru restaurant sells to people inside a short drive-time ring, so any hold case that needs demographic growth is underwriting against the data.

Sources: QuickFacts: Los Angeles County, California; QuickFacts: Los Angeles city, California. Official projection values are withheld — see F-04.

4. The labour market looks better than it is

Metro unemployment was 4.4% in September 2026, but the civilian labour force also fell, from 6,768,502 to 6,646,903 — about 121,600 fewer people. Total nonfarm employment was 6,282.6 thousand in August 2026, up only 0.6% year on year. A falling unemployment rate caused by people leaving the labour force is not an expanding economy.

The tenant's own sector is one of the few growing: leisure and hospitality added jobs. That is a real positive, and it is outweighed by the brand-specific evidence in findings 1 and 2.

Sources: Economy at a Glance: Los Angeles-Long Beach-Santa Ana, CA (MSA); Los Angeles Area Economic Summary.

5. A statutory wage ratchet compresses the tenant's margin

California's fast-food minimum wage is $20.00/hour and applies regardless of whether the employer is the brand owner or a franchisee. The state general minimum wage is $16.90/hour and the City of Los Angeles general wage is $18.42/hour, so the $20.00 fast-food floor is the binding rate inside the city. The tenant cannot restructure its way out of it.

Sources: Fast Food Minimum Wage Frequently Asked Questions (AB 1228); Minimum Wage Frequently Asked Questions; Office of Wage Standards - minimum wage announcements.

6. The tail risk is already realised in this county

The January 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires burned 14,021 acres and 23,448 acres, destroyed 9,414 structures and 6,837 structures, and removed an estimated $5.2–10.1 billion of economic output. USGS puts the 30-year probability of a magnitude 6.7 or greater earthquake in the Los Angeles region at 60% probability; a hold to 2035 sits inside that window.

Sources: Key facts about the Eaton and Palisades Fires (anniversary fact sheet); What is the probability that an earthquake will occur in the Los Angeles area?; In Focus IF12871: January 2025 Los Angeles County wildfires. Conditions attached to this recommendation

  1. Establish, in writing and before spending anything, whether the site is inside the tenant's closure programme. One conversation; it decides whether the extension branch exists at all.
  2. Read the option clause. If renewal is at the tenant's sole discretion, as the issuer discloses for its own leases, renewing is not a decision the owner can make.
  3. If a corporate guaranty exists and the site is off the closure list, extending first then selling outranks an immediate sale on both base and expected value.
  4. If zoning confirms a materially better alternative use, hold-then-redevelop competes on expected value. That is a growth choice, made explicitly, with entitlement risk priced.
  5. Complete the Input Register before committing capital to any branch.

What would change the answer

  • Tract-level evidence that the trade area is growing against the county trend.
  • A confirmed corporate guaranty plus written confirmation the site is outside the closure programme.
  • An entitlement position making redevelopment clearly more valuable than modelled.
  • Exit cap rates below roughly 5.5%, which is where hold-and-re-let starts to beat selling in the pessimistic case.
  • Verified, affordable, replacement-cost insurance — which would reduce the black-swan deduction.
