The balance of verified evidence

What supports holding

  • Unemployment fell to 4.4% in the metro and 4.5% in the city and county. BLS ↗
  • Education and health services, the largest metro sector at 1,340.7 thousand jobs, grew 3.8%. BLS ↗
  • Median household income is $81,939 in the city and $90,112 in the county. Census ↗
  • Property tax assessment rules may favor holding; the actual assessed value and future changes are unknown. Assessor ↗

What argues for acting early

  • City population is 0.8% below its 2020 estimates base; the county is 3.2% below. Census ↗
  • The issuer reports −1.1% system same-store sales, 17 quarterly closures and FY2026 guidance of 50–60 closures. SEC ↗
  • CPI-U is +3.7% and the employment cost index +3.9%; a fixed escalator may not keep pace. BLS ↗
  • A City of LA sale above $5.4m can add a 4% ULA surcharge. City Finance ↗

Risk balance

Business as usual, then the tail

Business as usual

Income concentration. One tenant, one building, one lease. Vacancy removes the full rent while taxes, insurance and re-letting costs continue.

Wage and cost pressure. Covered fast-food work has a $20.00/hour floor; the City of LA general floor is $18.42/hour from 2026-07-01, and the state floor is $16.90/hour from 2026. Store-level rent coverage is unknown.

Rates and exit yields. A higher exit yield lowers price for the same rent.

Taxes. A qualifying §1031 exchange generally defers gain; eligibility and eventual tax depend on the facts.

Full risk register →

Tail events that can end the local economy

Wildfire. In January 2025 the Palisades and Eaton fires destroyed 16,264 structures in this county in 24 days. Insurance availability and pricing can change region-wide afterwards.

Earthquake. USGS models a plausible M7.8 southern San Andreas event at about 1,800 deaths and $213bn in regional losses. Parcel-level ground-failure exposure has not been checked.

Flood, correlated utility or pandemic disruption. No parcel zone or probability is assigned.

Tenant failure or accelerated closure. The issuer closes underperforming units and sells owned real estate; a chain lease is not permanent.
