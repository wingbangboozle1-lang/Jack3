Should this Jack in the Box net lease be sold before 2030,
or renewed? A sourced answer, and its limits.

A single-tenant NNN restaurant property in central Los Angeles with a lease reported to expire between 2030 and 2035. This dashboard collects the official evidence that bears on the decision, separates what is verified from what is judgement, and leaves blank what neither can supply.

Conditional recommendation · analyst judgement, not a valuation

Run a dual track now: take the asset quietly to market, and demand renewal terms in writing in the same quarter.

Take a confidential sale process to market 24–36 months before the earliest possible expiry, and in parallel ask the tenant for rent, escalators, options, expense caps and a guaranty. Then act on whichever the documents support: a verified after-tax bid, or an enforceable extension of ten years or more at market rent with acceptable credit. What is not defensible is letting an option notice window pass on regional averages. At expiry the landlord has no income and the tenant has no obligation — leverage belongs to whoever can wait, and that is the tenant.

Why this is the balance point

Business as usual, then the tail

What the verified record shows

  • Demand is contracting slowly. City population is 0.8% below its 2020 base and the county 3.2% below, while payrolls grew 0.5% — less than the 3.7% local inflation rate.
  • The tenant is shrinking its own system. 17 closures against 4 openings in one quarter, a 150–200 unit closure programme, and about 1% of units a year thereafter.
  • Costs are rising faster than a typical escalator. Local compensation costs rose 4.2% and wages 3.9% in the year to September 2026; the subject lease's escalator is unknown.
  • Exit pricing is exposed to rates. The 10-year Treasury is 4.88%. A move from a 6.0% to an 8.0% exit cap costs a quarter of the value for the same rent.
  • The sale is expensive here. Transfer taxes alone take 4.56% of a $6.0m city sale, before commission and tax on gain.

What would change the answer

  • A signed extension at market rent, ten years or longer, with a verified creditworthy guarantor.
  • A verified after-tax bid above the risk-adjusted value of holding — a real offer, not an appraisal.
  • Block-level evidence that this trade area is growing, rather than city or county averages.
  • A change in the owner's own position: liquidity need, concentration, estate timing.

None of these can be established from the public record collected here. The property's identity, rent, basis, debt and lease are not public documents, and this project deliberately does not hold them.

How to read this file

The method in four lines
Only official pages

Government agencies, statutes and the tenant's own securities filings. No aggregators, no brokers, no estimates attributed to "market sources".

Every row carries its link

Each value points at the exact page it was read from, with the geography and period beside it. The validator fails the build if a row has no source.

Blank beats invented

If a value cannot be confirmed on an official page it is left blank, marked unverified, and published on the review page with the check that would close it.

Judgement is labelled

Analysis rows are this project's reasoning. They are never counted as verified and never presented as a statistic.

Independent decision-support research on public sources. Not an appraisal, not legal, tax or investment advice, and not a personalised recommendation. Asset A and Owner A are placeholders. Every published value carries a link to the official page it came from; anything that could not be confirmed is blank and listed on Flagged for review.
