DECISION FILE · CENTRAL LOS ANGELES · NET LEASE

Sell, renew — or extend first and then sell?

A Jack in the Box net-leased restaurant in central Los Angeles, reported to expire between 2030 and 2035. This file reads only official sources, publishes the receipt for every number, and leaves blank what cannot be proved.

CONDITIONAL RECOMMENDATION · ANALYST JUDGEMENT, NOT A VALUATION

Open renewal negotiations now. If the tenant will commit to ten years or more at market rent, sell the lengthened lease. If it will not, sell before expiry.

The only outcome to avoid is letting a 2030–2035 option clock run out with no written answer from the tenant. That route scores last on its worst case in the model on this site.

The trade, in one paragraph

What the verified evidence says

The regional economy is flat, not collapsing. Metro payrolls grew 0.5% year over year and unemployment fell a full point, but the county has 3.2% fewer residents than in 2020 and the state projects a further 1.5% decline by 2035.

The tenant is shrinking deliberately. Jack in the Box closed 17 restaurants in one quarter, guided to 50–60 closures for the year, discontinued its dividend, and said the rest of a 150–200 unit closure programme would close at franchise-agreement termination dates. A 2030–2035 lease expiry is exactly that kind of date.

The exit is taxed twice. A City of Los Angeles sale above $5.4 million pays a 4.45% city transfer tax, and the gain is taxed federally and again in California, which gives capital gains no preferential rate.

What that implies for the decision

A net-lease buyer is paying for a contractual income stream. Its value comes from the term remaining, the credit behind it, and the rent. Extending the lease is the only action available to the owner that lengthens the stream without selling.

Because the rent was never disclosed, this file does not assert a dollar value. It values every strategy in units of R, one year of current base rent, and ranks them on the worst case.

Risk balance

Business as usual

  • Income concentration. One tenant, one building. Vacancy costs the full rent plus taxes, insurance and re-letting.
  • Wage-floor pressure. Fast-food work pays $20.00/hour under AB 1228; the city floor is $18.42 and the state floor $16.90.
  • Rates. A 10-year Treasury at 4.86% sets the floor under a buyer's required return. Higher yields mean lower prices.
  • Taxes. Measure ULA adds 4% of gross above $5.4 million, and the gain is taxed again at exit.

Tail events

  • Earthquake. USGS puts the Los Angeles region at 60% for M≥6.7 within 30 years and models a plausible M7.8 at ~1,800 deaths and $213 billion in losses.
  • Wildfire. Two January 2025 fires destroyed 16,264 structures in this county. Insurance availability, not the flame front, is the exposure for an urban pad.
  • Flood and ground failure. FEMA and CGS are the official maps; the parcel zone is unknown and left blank.
  • Tenant failure. The issuer's own plan closes stores and sells real estate. It is not a guarantee for this site.
