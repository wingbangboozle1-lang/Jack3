Projection Model

A reproducible, deterministic model that compares every available strategy for PROPERTY-01. It is written so that it needs no private property data to produce a defensible ranking — and it says so when it is guessing.

Read this before any number below

The owner's actual lease economics were never supplied. Rather than invent a rent, a basis or a building size, the model is written in units of R = one year of the property's current contractual base rent. Every output is a dimensionless multiple of R.

This is not a workaround — it is the right form for this decision. Sell-versus-hold compares two cash-flow streams from the same asset, so the rent level largely cancels out of the ranking. The owner reads “strategy X returns 15.28 R versus strategy Y at 6.78 R” and multiplies by their own actual rent in private. No private figure has to be published for the analysis to work.

Declared assumptions

Every one of these is a guess, and is labelled as one

No assumption below is verified data. Each carries a low, base and high value and a favourability direction — which extreme is good for the owner. Without the direction, an “optimistic” scenario silently mixes optimistic and pessimistic inputs, which is the most common way a sensitivity grid lies. The optimistic scenario takes the favourable extreme of every assumption; the pessimistic scenario takes the unfavourable extreme of every one.

F-02 — cap rates cannot be verified

No United States government agency publishes commercial capitalisation rates or comparable sale prices for single-tenant net-lease restaurants. That data is private and sold by commercial vendors. The two cap-rate assumptions above are therefore swept across a deliberately wide band (5.0% to 8.5%) and every valuation conclusion on this site is presented as a range. Any adviser quoting a single precise market value is quoting a private dataset, not an official statistic.

Strategies evaluated

S1 — Sell now, lease in place

Market the asset today with the Jack in the Box NNN lease in place, priced off in-place income.

Wealth preservation: Highest - converts the position to cash at the point of maximum remaining term.

Growth: Lowest - forfeits all future upside.

Key dependency: A buyer willing to underwrite a shortening remaining term.

How the model prices it: Priced at the 'today' cap rate less transaction friction. Highest worst-case present value of any strategy in the model.

S2 — Hold to expiry, then sell vacant

Collect contractual rent to 2030-2035, then sell the empty box and land.

Wealth preservation: Medium - full rent to expiry, but terminal value carries all the location risk.

Growth: Low.

Key dependency: The alternative-use value of the land at the expiry date.

How the model prices it: Terminal value taken as the LOWER of a capitalised replacement rent and the alternative-use value, so the model never assumes both.

S3 — Renew / re-let to Jack in the Box

Grant a new long term to the incumbent brand at expiry.

Wealth preservation: Lowest - the model ranks this last in both the base and pessimistic scenarios.

Growth: Low - renews into a declining unit count and a declining county population.

Key dependency: Entirely on tenant credit and tenant willingness. Both are outside the owner's control and both are currently deteriorating.

How the model prices it: Discounted by the probability the tenant is still trading and still wants the site, then charged re-tenanting capital and vacancy. This double hit is why it ranks last.

S4 — Hold to expiry, re-let to a different operator

Let the Jack in the Box term run out and reposition the box with another quick-service or retail user.

Wealth preservation: Medium-low - the highest expected value but the lowest floor of the strong strategies.

Growth: Highest of the re-letting strategies.

Key dependency: Demand for second-generation drive-thru boxes in the specific trade area.

How the model prices it: Longer downtime and higher capital than S3 because a non-branded box needs more adaptation, but no tenant-survival discount.

S5 — Negotiate an early extension, then sell

Buy additional term from the tenant now (at a rent concession), then sell the lengthened lease.

Wealth preservation: High - best mean rank across the three scenarios.

Growth: Low.

Key dependency: Tenant willingness to extend while it is running a closure programme. This is the weak link.

How the model prices it: Cap-rate compression from the longer term is traded against a rent concession; both are declared assumptions and both are swept.

S6 — Hold to expiry, then redevelop

Take the site back at expiry and pursue a higher-and-better use.

Wealth preservation: Medium - the most robust of the hold strategies in the base case.

Growth: Highest absolute upside.

Key dependency: Zoning, entitlement, and whether Central Los Angeles land values support a better use than a drive-thru.

How the model prices it: Terminal value is the full alternative-use value with no capitalised-rent cap, which is why it outperforms S2 whenever that value is high.

Reproduce

python3 -m model.projection prints every scenario and every strategy ranked by risk-adjusted present value in R. The model is deterministic: same inputs, same outputs, no randomness, no network calls. Source: model/projection.py.
