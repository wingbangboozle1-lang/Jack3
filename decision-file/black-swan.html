Black Swan and Tail-Event Register

Events that could destroy the local economy or the asset itself. Business-as-usual risk is on the Risk Register; this page is for the events that are rare, severe, and — in two cases here — already realised.

One of these is not a tail. It already happened.

The Eaton and Palisades Fires of January 2025 burned 37,469 acres between them and destroyed 16,251 structures in Los Angeles County. The County's own fact sheet puts economic output lost at $5.2 to $10.1 billion, with approximately 6,800 businesses and 47,000 workers impacted and tax revenue losses of $900 million to $1.6 billion. For a Los Angeles property, wildfire is a realised hazard with a measured economic cost, not a hypothetical.

Transmission mechanisms — how each event reaches the owner's return

B-01 — Major wind-driven wildfire in the trade area

Transmission to value: Direct physical loss; prolonged rebuild period; insurance repricing across the whole county raises the tenant's NNN cost and the buyer's underwriting; trade-area demand displaced for years.

Precedent: REALISED 2025-01-07: Eaton and Palisades Fires, Los Angeles County

B-02 — Magnitude 6.7 or greater earthquake in the Los Angeles region

Transmission to value: Physical damage to the building; business interruption; a buyer will not finance an unreinforced structure; insurance may become unavailable at any price.

Precedent: 1994 Northridge earthquake (magnitude 6.7) is the reference event used by USGS

B-03 — Tenant brand-level failure or a large-scale closure wave

Transmission to value: Loss of the income stream; the box becomes a second-generation restaurant asset in a market where the largest operator is contracting; re-letting requires a different tenant entirely.

Precedent: Disclosed and in progress: the JACK on Track block closure programme

B-04 — Riverine or debris-flow flooding after wildfire-denuded slopes

Transmission to value: Damage to ground-floor commercial property; insurance exclusions; prolonged access disruption.

Precedent: The January 2025 fires were followed by flash-flood warnings in the burn areas

B-05 — Extreme heat event suppressing drive-thru and dine-in traffic

Transmission to value: Short-term revenue suppression; longer-term labour availability; higher tenant utility cost under NNN.

Precedent: Heat wave is a rated hazard for Los Angeles County in FEMA's National Risk Index

B-06 — Sharp regional out-migration accelerating beyond official projections

Transmission to value: Structural erosion of the customer base and of exit values; every successive official forecast has been revised down, so the risk is asymmetric to the downside.

Precedent: Observed: -3.2% county population between the 2020 estimates base and the 2025 estimate

B-07 — Insurance market withdrawal from California commercial property

Transmission to value: If the building cannot be insured, the NNN lease cannot be performed and the asset cannot be financed by a buyer. This is a total-loss mechanism that does not require any physical damage.

Precedent: Not verified from an official source in this session

The insurance mechanism deserves separate emphasis

B-07 is the one tail that requires no physical damage at all. If commercial property insurance becomes unavailable or unaffordable for this building type in this county, the NNN lease cannot be performed and no institutional buyer will finance the asset. That is a total-loss mechanism arriving through a contract, not through a fire. It is marked UNVERIFIED because no official source was retrieved for it in this session — but it should be checked with the California Department of Insurance before any hold strategy is relied upon.
