Why each risk matters, and what to do about it

R-16 — The subject property was never identified, so all site-specific conclusions are necessarily incomplete.

Category: Analysis integrity   Risk score: 25   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: This is the largest single limitation on the whole analysis and it is disclosed rather than papered over. Site-level trade area, traffic counts, zoning, fire zone, flood zone and school catchment cannot be determined without it.

Mitigation: Complete the Input Register on the Property Profile page. Every field is a placeholder by design. Once supplied privately, the model in model/projection.py runs unchanged - it needs no re-engineering.

R-01 — The tenant's brand operator is running a disclosed 150-200 restaurant closure programme and has stated the remaining closures happen 'based upon respective franchise agreement termination dates'.

Category: Tenant credit   Risk score: 20   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A lease expiry date is precisely the trigger the closure programme uses. The 2030-2035 window is therefore not a neutral date - it is the date at which the tenant's own stated process evaluates whether to keep the site.

Mitigation: Confirm whether the site is on the operator's closure list before spending anything on renewal negotiation. Require a corporate guaranty or a letter of credit as a renewal condition. Begin exit preparation now rather than at expiry.

R-02 — System same-store sales have declined in every recent reporting period.

Category: Tenant credit   Risk score: 20   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: Store-level sales drive a franchisee's ability to pay rent and its willingness to renew. Sustained negative comps convert into rent concessions, closures, or non-renewal.

Mitigation: Obtain the subject restaurant's actual sales history and rent-to-sales ratio. A rent-to-sales ratio materially above sector norms is the strongest single predictor of non-renewal.

R-07 — Official long-range projections disagree on the DIRECTION of travel for the county's population.

Category: Market / demand   Risk score: 20   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A 25-year horizon decision cannot be made on a growth assumption when the two official planning bodies disagree on the sign. Any plan that requires population growth to work is not robust.

Mitigation: Underwrite to the OBSERVED trend (Census Vintage 2025 actuals), not to either forecast. Treat growth as upside, never as the base case.

R-13 — No U.S. government agency publishes commercial capitalisation rates or comparable sale prices, so the exit value cannot be verified from an official source.

Category: Valuation   Risk score: 20   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: The single most important number in a sell-versus-hold decision cannot be officially verified. Any adviser quoting a precise value is quoting a private dataset, not an official statistic.

Mitigation: Commission an MAI appraisal. Treat every cap-rate-dependent conclusion as a range and check which side of the range flips the decision.

R-03 — Franchisee profitability is under disclosed pressure, and the operator has said franchise closures reduce franchise-level margin by roughly $80,000 each.

Category: Tenant credit   Risk score: 16   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: In an NNN structure the landlord's counterparty is usually the franchisee, not the corporate. Franchisee distress is direct counterparty distress.

Mitigation: Verify whether the lease carries a Jack in the Box Inc. corporate guaranty. If it does not, treat the credit as a small-business credit, not a NASDAQ-listed credit.

R-17 — Holding concentrates the owner's wealth in one illiquid single-tenant asset in one county.

Category: Opportunity cost   Risk score: 16   Evidence class: JUDGEMENT

Why it matters: The brief asks for preservation of wealth AND opportunity for growth at a manageable level of risk. A single concentrated position fails the diversification test regardless of how the individual numbers look.

Mitigation: Selling converts one illiquid correlated position into capital that can be diversified. This is a reason to sell that is independent of the cap-rate view.

R-04 — California's fast-food minimum wage floor is $20.00/hour and is set by a statutory council with authority to raise it further through 2029.

Category: Tenant cost   Risk score: 15   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A statutory wage floor the tenant cannot avoid compresses the tenant's margin, which compresses the rent it can pay at renewal. This is a structural, not cyclical, pressure.

Mitigation: Model renewal rent against the tenant's demonstrated rent-to-sales ratio rather than against market comparables.

R-05 — If the tenant vacates at expiry, a single-tenant drive-thru box in an infill urban location is slow and expensive to re-let.

Category: Rollover / vacancy   Risk score: 15   Evidence class: MODELLED

Why it matters: This is the largest single swing factor in the model. It is also the factor the owner controls least.

Mitigation: Line up a replacement tenant or a redevelopment path BEFORE the expiry notice date. A site with an identified alternative use is worth materially more than one marketed cold.

R-06 — Los Angeles County population is falling, not growing.

Category: Market / demand   Risk score: 15   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: Quick-service restaurant volume is a function of population within a drive-time ring. A shrinking base erodes transaction counts, which is exactly what the tenant is reporting.

Mitigation: Do not underwrite renewal on an assumption of demographic growth. Test the trade area's own trajectory at tract level once the address is known.

R-12 — Earthquake exposure over the hold period is material and cannot be mitigated by timing.

Category: Catastrophe   Risk score: 15   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A 60% 30-year probability of an M6.7+ event overlaps almost entirely with a hold-to-2035 decision. Every additional year of holding adds exposure that no rent increase compensates for.

Mitigation: Verify the building's construction type and any seismic retrofit. Insurance availability and cost for earthquake is a first-order question, not a footnote.

R-14 — A sale crystallises tax and friction that a hold does not, and California's treatment of deferred gain is unusual.

Category: Tax / transaction   Risk score: 15   Evidence class: JUDGEMENT

Why it matters: Net proceeds, not gross price, are what the owner keeps. A 4.5% friction assumption in the model is a placeholder, not advice.

Mitigation: Obtain a written tax opinion from a California-licensed CPA or tax attorney covering gain, depreciation recapture, exchange eligibility, and withholding BEFORE signing anything. See the Tax Framework page for the official sources to bring to that conversation.

R-08 — Employment growth in the metro is weak and unevenly distributed across sectors.

Category: Employment   Risk score: 12   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A +0.5% headline masks a two-speed economy. Daypart traffic for a quick-service restaurant depends on which sectors are near the site.

Mitigation: Identify the dominant employment sectors within a 1-3 mile ring once the address is known, and check them against this sector table.

R-09 — The civilian labour force is shrinking even while the unemployment rate falls.

Category: Employment   Risk score: 12   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: A falling unemployment rate that is caused by people leaving the labour force is not an improving economy. Fewer workers and fewer residents means fewer customers.

Mitigation: Read the unemployment improvement together with the labour-force level, never alone.

R-10 — Local inflation is running above the national average and energy costs are rising sharply.

Category: Inflation / cost   Risk score: 12   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: Under NNN the tenant pays the operating costs, so inflation is transferred to the tenant - which is good for the landlord until it breaks the tenant. Energy at +20.3% (12 months to Sep 2026) is a direct hit on a 24-hour drive-thru operation.

Mitigation: Confirm the lease is genuinely absolute NNN with no landlord residual exposure to taxes, insurance or structure.

R-11 — Wind-driven wildfire has already destroyed a large share of structures in Los Angeles County within the last two years.

Category: Catastrophe   Risk score: 10   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: This is not a theoretical tail. It happened in this county 22 months ago and removed up to 1% of annual county economic output. It also feeds directly into insurance cost and availability, which affects both the tenant and any buyer.

Mitigation: Determine the parcel's CAL FIRE Fire Hazard Severity Zone (2025 re-mapping applies). Confirm insurance is placed and renewable. A buyer will ask; an uninsured or uninsurable site does not sell.

R-15 — Tenant leadership instability.

Category: Governance   Risk score: 6   Evidence class: VERIFIED

Why it matters: Counterparty strategy is less predictable during leadership transition, which matters when negotiating a renewal several years out.

Mitigation: Do not rely on informal or relationship-based assurances from the tenant. Anything that matters must be in the lease document.
