Methodology and Verification Protocol

How every figure in this file was obtained, what was accepted as evidence, what was rejected, and how the reader can check any of it.

Evidence classes

The six evidence classes used across the site

LabelMeaningDefinition and handling
VERIFIEDAccepted asRead directly on an official .gov page, or in a filing the issuer made with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, during this analysis session. Every VERIFIED row carries the exact URL it was read from.
MODELLEDTreated asAn arithmetic output of model/projection.py computed from declared assumptions. Reproducible, but not an observation of the world.

Source hierarchy applied

  1. Primary federal and state agencies. SEC EDGAR, U.S. Census Bureau, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, U.S. Geological Survey, U.S. Department of the Treasury, FEMA, CAL FIRE, California Department of Industrial Relations, California Department of Finance, Congressional Research Service.
  2. County and municipal governments. County of Los Angeles, City of Los Angeles Office of Wage Standards, City of Los Angeles Economic and Workforce Development Department, Los Angeles County Fire Department open data.
  3. The issuer itself. Jack in the Box Inc. earnings release — accepted only because the same figures were furnished to the SEC on Form 8-K the same day.
  4. Rejected outright. Commercial brokerage listings, net-lease aggregator sites, investor forums, media restatements of government data, and any third-party restatement of a figure that could be read at source. Where a third-party figure was the only one obtainable, the value is left blank and the official route is given instead.
Three concrete cases where the protocol changed the answer

Minimum wage. Third-party sources gave $17.00 for the California rate. The California Department of Industrial Relations page gives $16.90 effective 1 January 2026. The official figure was used and the conflict recorded.

Population projections. Two official bodies disagree on the direction of travel for Los Angeles County. Rather than average them or pick the convenient one, both are published side by side and the analysis underwrites to the Census Bureau's measured −3.2% instead.

Cap rates. Private net-lease sites publish Jack in the Box cap rates. None were used. Valuation is a sensitivity grid across an explicit band, and the absence of an official source is itself recorded as a finding.

Verification mechanics

  • Every external URL emitted into docs/ is extracted and checked by tools/check_links.py, which reports malformed URLs, duplicates, and any non-official domain appearing in a column that claims to be verified.
  • Every page is scanned by tools/pii_scan.py for personal identifiers — names, email addresses, street addresses, parcel numbers, telephone numbers and entity names that could identify the owner or the tenant operator. The build fails if any are found.
  • The site is static HTML with no runtime data fetching. Published figures cannot change after generation, so what a reviewer reads is what was verified.
  • Each row carries its own as of date because the snapshot mixes Census Vintage 2025 estimates, ACS 2020–2024 five-year averages, the 2022 Economic Census, BLS August and September 2026 data, and SEC filings for periods ending September 2025 and July 2026. The Census Bureau itself warns that estimates from different sources and vintages are not comparable.

Known limitations of this method

  • The U.S. Census Bureau Data API requires a key, which is unavailable here, so demographic data was read from the server-rendered QuickFacts pages instead. That limits the analysis to the QuickFacts variable set; tract-level ACS tables were not reachable.
  • California Department of Finance projections are published only as spreadsheet files on a domain this environment cannot reach. The projected values are blank.
  • Two official tax pages returned HTTP 404 when retrieved, so no tax rate is asserted anywhere on the site.
  • Because the parcel is unidentified, no trade-area, traffic-count, zoning, fire-zone, flood-zone or school-catchment analysis is possible at site level. The analysis is at market and submarket level and says so.
