Why the FEMA county average is the wrong number to use

FEMA's National Risk Index is published down to census-tract level, and wildfire loss in Los Angeles County is extremely concentrated: most tracts have effectively none, while a small minority carry very large expected losses. A county average therefore tells an owner almost nothing about a specific parcel. The correct action is to query the subject tract in FEMA's Resilience Analysis and Planning Tool once the address is known.

CAL FIRE re-mapped Los Angeles County in 2025

Los Angeles County Fire Department Local Responsibility Area Fire Hazard Severity Zones were established in 2025, and the 2025 revision adds Moderate and High categories where the previous map showed only Very High. Any fire-zone determination taken from a pre-2025 map, a title report or an old insurance submission is stale. Separately, State Responsibility Area mapping has not been updated since 1985 except for boundary changes.
