Decision Framework

The recommendation, the reasoning, the conditions under which it changes, and what evidence would overturn it.

Recommendation
Sell - and if the tenant will extend first, extend, then sell.

Selling now has the highest worst-case present value of every strategy modelled. Renewing the Jack in the Box lease ranks last in both the base and the pessimistic scenario. The tenant’s own SEC filings describe a 150–200 restaurant closure programme in which the remaining closures occur “based upon respective franchise agreement termination dates” — and 2030–2035 is that date.

Why

  1. The model's highest worst-case present value belongs to selling now (11.62 R in the pessimistic scenario, against 9.88 R for the next-best strategy and 1.18 R for renewing the Jack in the Box lease). Under the owner's stated objective - preservation of wealth at a manageable level of risk - the floor matters more than the ceiling, and selling has the highest floor.
  2. Renewing the Jack in the Box lease ranks LAST in both the base and pessimistic scenarios. It is the only strategy that is heavily discounted by tenant-survival probability, because it is the only one whose entire value depends on the incumbent still wanting the site.
  3. The tenant's own SEC filings describe a 150-200 restaurant closure programme in which the remaining closures occur 'based upon respective franchise agreement termination dates'. A 2030-2035 expiry is that date. Renewing means underwriting the assumption that this specific site is not on that list, with no evidence either way.
  4. Los Angeles County population is measured to have fallen 3.2% since the 2020 estimates base, and the two official planning bodies disagree on the sign of the long-range forecast. A hold strategy that needs demographic growth to work is not robust.
  5. The brand is itself a net seller of restaurant real estate: the FY2025 10-K describes sale and sale-leaseback programmes and offering franchisees the chance to buy the property they occupy. Seller-side liquidity exists, and the owner is not the only one exiting.
  6. Selling also resolves the concentration problem. It converts one illiquid, single-tenant, single-county position into diversifiable capital - a benefit that is independent of any view on cap rates.

Conditions attached to this recommendation

Condition

CONDITION: before spending anything on an extension negotiation, establish whether the subject site is inside the tenant's closure programme. If it is, S5 is unavailable and S1 is the answer outright.

Condition

CONDITION: if the lease carries a Jack in the Box Inc. corporate guaranty and the site is confirmed to be off the closure list, S5 (extend then sell) becomes preferable to S1, because it sells a longer and more financeable income stream.

Condition

CONDITION: if the land has a demonstrably better alternative use - confirmed by zoning, not by hope - then S6 outranks S1 on expected value and the owner is choosing growth over preservation. That is a legitimate choice, but it should be made explicitly and with entitlement risk priced.

Condition

CONDITION: complete the Input Register on the Property Profile page first. Every strategy ranking above is normalised to R because the actual rent, basis and building details were never supplied.

What would change the answer

  • Evidence that the trade area is growing against the county trend - a tract-level population and income gain that contradicts the county aggregate.
  • A confirmed corporate guaranty plus written confirmation from the tenant that the site is not in the closure programme.
  • A zoning or entitlement position that makes S6 materially more valuable than the modelled alternative-use range.
  • Cap rates for this asset type tightening far enough that a hold-and-re-let strategy beats selling in the pessimistic scenario. On the model, that requires the exit cap rate to fall below roughly 5.5%.
Balancing preservation against growth, as the brief asks

The two objectives pull in different directions and the model quantifies the trade. Selling now maximises the floor: 11.62 R in the worst case, the highest of any strategy, with the narrowest spread. Holding to expiry and re-letting to a different operator maximises the weighted expected value at 17.15 R, but its worst case is 4.82 R — a spread more than three times wider. That is the growth option, and it is only appropriate if the owner can absorb the downside without distress and has independently confirmed a demand for the box from a tenant other than Jack in the Box.

A middle path exists and is modelled as S5: negotiate an early extension with the tenant, then sell the lengthened lease. It has the best mean rank across the three scenarios (6th, 1st, 2nd) and the second-highest floor. Its weak link is that it requires a tenant that is publicly contracting to agree to a longer commitment.
