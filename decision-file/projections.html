Official Population and Economic Forecasts

The lease decision spans 2030 to 2035. This page collects what the official forecasting bodies actually publish for that horizon — and discloses, plainly, that they disagree with each other.

Two official bodies, opposite signs

SCAG, the federally designated Metropolitan Planning Organization for the region, projected Los Angeles County to grow 16.6% to 11,674,000 by 2045 in its Connect SoCal 2020 plan. The California Department of Finance projection cited in the City of Los Angeles 2024 workforce plan has the county falling to about 9.3 million by 2040 and 8.3 million by 2060. The only line in the table below that is measurement rather than forecast is the Census Bureau's observed −3.2% since 2020.

This analysis underwrites to the observed trend and treats growth as upside, never as the base case.

F-04 — the most important missing number

The California Department of Finance publishes county projections only as spreadsheet files (P2A_County_Total.xlsx), and this environment has no network route to dof.ca.gov. Third-party restatements of the same DOF series disagree by more than 100,000 residents for the same year across vintages. The projected county population for 2030, 2035 and 2040 is therefore left blank rather than approximated.

The 2035 value is the mid-point of the stated expiry window and is the single most useful forecast number for this decision. Download P-2A from the official Department of Finance page ↗ and read the Los Angeles row. Note the vintage: Vintage 2026, Baseline 2024.
