Sell Before Expiry — or Renew at Expiry?

The full anatomy of both paths: NNN value mechanics, tenant credit, sale friction under Measure ULA, 1031 strategy, and the exact triggers that should flip the decision from “renew” to “sell.”

1 · How a NNN Asset Is Valued Value = NOI ÷ cap rate

  • NNN lease: The Tenant pays property taxes, insurance and maintenance (OpEx) — owner receives essentially the rent check. Minimal landlord overhead, minimal control.
  • Valuation identity: Price = NOI ÷ cap rate. A $300,000/yr net rent at a 5.5% cap ≈ $5.45M. At 6.5% cap ≈ $4.62M. A 100bp cap-rate drift moves value ~17% — the single biggest price risk besides tenant credit.
  • Escalations: typical 1.0–2.0%/yr bumps; inflation hedge but far below market reset power (a renewal re-sets rent to market, an existing escalator does not).
  • Risk is tenant credit: NNN return ≈ credit spread of the tenant. JIB-franchisee paper is not investment grade in the bond sense; diligence it like a private-credit loan.
  • At expiry: two assets remain — (a) the real estate (land + improvements) and (b) the lease income. If renewed, both persist; if vacated, only (a) remains and value reprices to land/pad/dark-box value. This asymmetry is the whole decision.

Cliff strategy (honest version): sellers cluster listings just below $5.3M. A seller whose asset genuinely appraises at $5.25M lists there; a seller whose asset is worth $6M does NOT ethically “price at $5.3M” — they sell at market and pay 4%. Where ULA legally bites, options are: (a) 1031 into replacement property (does NOT avoid ULA — it’s a transfer tax, not a cap-gains tax), (b) restructure (entity/prop co splits — HIGH legal risk, Office of Finance anti-abuse rules apply; do not attempt without counsel), (c) negotiate seller-financing or leasehold sale to alter consideration (legal review required), (d) simply hold and renew — the cleanest ULA avoidance is not selling. Confirm exemptions with the LA Office of Finance in writing.

6 · Option Matrix — Sell vs Renew vs Hybrid

SELL BEFORE / AT EXPIRY Option A
  • Pros: exits Tenant credit & rollover risk; crystallizes equity; no 1031 clock if not exchanging; clean balance sheet
  • Cons: ULA 4–5.5% + DTT + commissions (~7–9% all-in); loses escalating NNN cash flow; cap-rate market at 12-yr highs; buyer re-underwrites Tenant → price cut; Prop 13 reset suppresses demand
  • Best when: Tenant will NOT extend; unit is in closure program; offers ≥ 6.25% cap clear hurdle; trigger fires ≥18 mo pre-expiry
  • Wealth impact: preserves principal, forfeits compounding income; 1031 required to avoid tax leakage
RENEW & HOLD Option B
  • Pros: zero transaction friction; Prop 13 basis kept; NNN income at ~5–6% unlevered yield +1–2%/yr bumps; DTLA 2040 upside optionality free; 1031 later (always an option)
  • Cons: holds Tenant credit (SSS negative, closure program); escalation may lag inflation vs reset risk; black-swan residual (seismic/fire/unrest/insurance)
  • Best when: Tenant signs ≥24 mo pre-expiry; unit coverage >1.25× rent; escalation ≥2%; guarantor strength adequate
  • Wealth impact: compounding income + optionality; highest risk-adjusted outcome if Tenant performs
Hybrid (recommended): Option B with Option A pre-authorized. Negotiate extension now; if unsigned at T-24 months or any trigger fires, convert to Option A immediately — while the NNN rent stream is still a bankable asset for buyers. This preserves the option value of both paths and caps the left tail.
