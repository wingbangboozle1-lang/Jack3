Method & privacy

Method

How the verification standard works, why the model is built the way it is, and what the numbers actually mean.

What 'verified' means here

A value is marked verified only if it was read on the publishing agency's own domain — federalreserve.gov, home.treasury.gov, sec.gov, bls.gov, edd.ca.gov, census.gov, dof.ca.gov, dir.ca.gov, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, fire.ca.gov or irs.gov. Values that come from a government body but were read through a reproduction are marked verified (secondary) and carry the caveat on the source record. Values that only appear in commercial or media sources are marked unverified, are blanked out of the tables, and are published on the Flagged for Review page with their links.

Why the model is scale-invariant

The property's price, rent, basis and lease terms are private and were never supplied. Rather than invent them, the engine expresses current rent as a going-in cap rate on value and reports every result as a return on the capital employed. Three property-value bands are run ($4.0M, $6.5M, $12.0M) specifically because they straddle the two Measure ULA thresholds, so the tax cliff is visible rather than averaged away. The ranking of strategies is stable across all three bands, which is the point: the conclusion does not depend on knowing the property's price.

Why terminal wealth rather than IRR

A raw IRR cannot compare a 2026 exit with a 2052 exit — it ignores what happens to the cash in between. The primary metric is therefore the compound annual return on deployed capital measured at a common horizon, with all interim cash reinvested, the property eventually liquidated, and all taxes paid including tax on reinvestment growth. A secondary metric reports net after-tax sale proceeds in the exit year, because that is the figure that determines whether a replacement asset can be bought.

Risk adjustment

Outcomes are ranked on three measures: the 5th-percentile terminal wealth, the mean of the worst 5% of outcomes (conditional value at risk), and a CRRA certainty equivalent at risk aversion 2 and 4. The certainty equivalent is the guaranteed amount a risk-averse owner would accept instead of the gamble, so it answers the user's actual brief — a manageable level of risk in exchange for growth — rather than simply maximising the median.

Reproducibility

The Monte Carlo is seeded (seed 20261106) and drawn entirely from the Python standard library. Re-running python3 tools/model.py reproduces every figure on the Projection page exactly. That is deliberate: the model's job is to be checkable, not impressive.

Privacy protocol

Personal information is the highest-priority constraint in this file. Here is exactly how it was handled.

Placeholders only

The property is referred to as SP-01 throughout. No street address, parcel number (AIN), owner name, tenant-unit identity, rent figure, lease date beyond the supplied window, or contact detail appears anywhere in this repository, its data files, its build scripts, its commit history, or this site.

Analysis at submarket granularity

Every dataset is county, city or metro level. The decision was deliberately built at that granularity so that the analysis carries no parcel-level information that could be used to re-identify the asset. The trade-off is real and is disclosed: trade-area demand, traffic counts, school catchment and zoning cannot be evaluated without the address, and those rows are blank and flagged rather than guessed.

Personal financial data was never requested

The owner's basis, capital gains bracket, ownership structure and debt position are unknown. Rather than ask for them, the model exposes each as a stated scenario parameter with a range, and reports results across that range. The sensitivity table shows which of these actually change the answer.

What would change if the address were supplied

Parcel size and zoning would fix redevelopment yield; the fire hazard severity zone and FEMA flood zone would fix hazard exposure; traffic counts and trade-area population would fix demand; the school catchment would fix the family-formation signal. Each of these is listed in the Site-Specific Variables table with a blank value and a review flag. None of them changes the ranking of the seven strategies, because that ranking is driven by tax and lease term rather than by site characteristics.
