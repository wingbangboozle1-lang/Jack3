Risk & black swans

Six event classes, grounded in verified events, and how each transmits into this asset's value.

Two kinds of risk, treated separately

Business as usual covers recessions, rent drift, cap-rate movement and tenant behaviour. It is modelled stochastically across 6,000 paths. Black swans are modelled as discrete named cases with an explicit annual probability (A13, 2.0%) and severity (A14, 35% of value) rather than buried in a standard deviation. Both are reported.

The black-swan register

Six event classes that could materially damage this asset or the local economy that supports it. Each is grounded in a verified event or a verified regulatory fact.

Wildfire reaching the urban core

Evidence. January 2025: 16,263 structures destroyed across the Palisades and Eaton fires; 29 confirmed civilian deaths; $9 billion removed from the 2026 assessment roll for wildfire relief (S21, S22, S25).

How it transmits to this asset. Rent interruption while the site is closed, smoke and ash remediation, utility outage, insurance repricing across the whole county, and a post-event cap-rate widening that hits every asset in the region regardless of whether it burned.

Modelled as. Case B3 applies a single-event shock in 2031. It costs the hold strategy about $6.8 million of terminal wealth on a $6.5 million asset.

What to check or do. Verify the parcel's fire hazard severity zone and brush-clearance obligation. Confirm the tenant's insurance is replacement-cost and that rent abatement is covered by business-interruption cover, not by the landlord.

Major seismic event

Evidence. Los Angeles County sits astride the southern San Andreas and a dense network of thrust faults. The specific 30-year probabilities could not be confirmed from a USGS endpoint during this session and are therefore published as unverified (F-04) rather than asserted.

How it transmits to this asset. Physical damage, mandatory retrofit, months of closure, and — critically — a county-wide insurance market shock. A quick-service box is a light-frame structure; the greater risk is the site's parking and canopy, and the tenant's ability to reopen.

Modelled as. Not separately modelled. Its economics resemble case B3 with a lower recovery ratio, because seismic damage is less uniformly insured than fire.

What to check or do. Obtain a seismic assessment. Establish whether the building is non-ductile concrete or soft-story, which would create a mandatory capital event.

Cap-rate dislocation

Evidence. Long-end Treasury yields are 4.84% at 10 years while the policy rate is 3.75–4.00%, and two FOMC members dissented in favour of raising rates (S01, S02). The term premium is positive and rising.

How it transmits to this asset. Net-lease cap rates are priced off the long end. A 175bp widening compresses asset value by roughly a quarter with no change in rent.

Modelled as. Case B4 applies +175bp. It costs the 10-year hold about $3.6 million of terminal wealth and makes the immediate §1031 exit the better choice.

What to check or do. This is the strongest argument for selling into strength. It is also the reason not to sell for cash — a dislocation is precisely when you want deferral, not a tax bill.

Tenant insolvency or strategic exit from the market

Evidence. The tenant closed 86 units in fiscal 2025 and guided to 50–100 more in fiscal 2026; it sold Del Taco; it discontinued its dividend; its CEO is interim; and its franchise-level margin fell to 37.4% with bad-debt expense rising (S12, S13).

How it transmits to this asset. Rejection of a renewal option, followed by dark rent for 9–36 months, tenant improvements, and a leasing commission — about 7.5% of value plus lost rent (A06, A07). A dark box also invites vandalism and municipal nuisance enforcement.

Modelled as. Case B2 assumes vacancy at expiry. It costs the hold strategy about $1.0 million. That is modest only because the site has strong residual land value; the real cost is the re-leasing calendar, which the model cannot observe.

What to check or do. Confirm the guarantor structure before renewal. A franchisee guarantee is not a corporate guarantee, and the model treats the difference as invisible.

Regulatory shock to the tenant's cost base

Evidence. California's fast-food minimum wage has been $20.00 per hour since April 2024, applying to any limited-service chain of 60 or more establishments nationwide, including franchisees. A standing Fast Food Council can raise it again at any time, and the general state minimum rose to $16.90 on 1 January 2026 with no tip credit permitted (S19, S20).

How it transmits to this asset. A wage floor that rises faster than menu prices compresses franchisee margin above the rent line. Margin compression is what precedes renewal refusal, not sales decline.

Modelled as. Not a discrete case; it enters through assumption A05 (renewal probability) and A12 (pricing-power offset).

What to check or do. Structure any renewal with fixed, contractually certain escalators rather than CPI linkage, so the landlord's income does not depend on the tenant's ability to pass through wage costs.

Fiscal and tax-base erosion

Evidence. Downtown office value declines and wildfire relief both subtracted from the 2026 roll, and the county applied the maximum 2% Proposition 13 inflation factor only because state CPI exceeded the cap (S15, S25). Los Angeles city assessed value is $926.1 billion of a $2.367 trillion county roll.

How it transmits to this asset. A weaker commercial tax base pushes the county and city toward higher rates, parcel taxes and fees — and toward Measure ULA-style instruments. Every incremental transfer tax lands directly on the owner's exit proceeds.

Modelled as. Not modelled as a discrete case, because the transfer-tax rates are statutory today. It is the reason to assume exit costs will be higher, not lower, in future.

What to check or do. Model exit on today's rates and treat the 2026 ULA thresholds as a floor. They are indexed to Chained CPI and will be higher in 2030.

The asymmetry that matters most

For an owner who intends to keep working capital intact, the relevant question is not the median outcome but the survival outcome. On a $6.5 million asset the mean of the worst 5% of outcomes is $14,467,825 for renew-and-hold, against $22,337,468 for an immediate §1031 exchange and $18,140,408 for a cash sale. The cash sale has the highest floor but the lowest ceiling by a wide margin, because the tax is paid with certainty and the growth is forgone with certainty. That is why the exchange structures dominate once risk aversion is taken seriously: they sit between the two extremes on every measure.
