Scenario model 2026-2035

One deterministic run, every assumption printed, and no dollar value of the asset anywhere. All results are multiples of one year of current base rent (1 R).

What this model is not. It is not a valuation, an appraisal, a price target or a forecast of rent, cap rates or returns. It does not predict whether the tenant renews or closes, and it assigns no probability to any hazard. It is arithmetic that shows which route wins under stated assumptions and how far those assumptions must move before the answer flips. Every input below marked ASSUMPTION is a judgement, not a published observation.

The headline result

Holding versus selling, by possible expiry year
At the base assumptions, selling today is worth 16.965 R net of costs, and holding to the last possible expiry (2035) is worth 16.269 R before tax, or 14.420 R if an 18-month void occurs at expiry. The hold case does not overtake the sell case at any expiry year tested: the ratio stays between 0.95 and 0.96 without vacancy, and between 0.82 and 0.85 with it. That is the arithmetic basis for the recommendation, and it is conditional on the assumptions above.

Break-even analysis

The number the owner actually needs to negotiate against

Column 2 is the annual rent growth that makes holding exactly equal selling, after transfer and selling costs. Column 3 repeats it with an 18-month void at expiry. Column 4 is the exit cap at which the sale proceeds would have to be priced to make the two routes equal: if the owner believes the exit cap will be below that figure, holding wins; if above, selling wins. Compare the required exit cap with the entry cap: the gap is the cap-rate expansion the market would have to avoid.

Why no dollar value is published

The calibration problem, stated plainly

One year of rent is unknown, so every output above is a ratio. Multiplying any row by a rent figure would produce a dollar number, but that rent figure does not exist in the public record. Publishing a dollar value would therefore require inventing the single most important input. The model instead reports what does not depend on scale: the ordering of routes, the break-even growth rate and the break-even exit cap.

Reproduce with python3 scripts/scenario_engine.py. The module runs its own self-test first: it checks the closed-form annuity against a brute-force year-by-year loop and confirms that each break-even solves back to the target exactly. If the self-test fails, the build fails.
