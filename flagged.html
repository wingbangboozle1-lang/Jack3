F-01 high Unverified — site specific

The subject property cannot be verified, by design

What was found. Sixteen decision-critical inputs are unknown because supplying them would disclose personal information: parcel size, building area, year built, contract rent, escalation structure, renewal option terms and deadlines, exact expiry date, whether the lease is genuinely triple-net, guarantor structure, zoning, trade-area population at 1/3/5 miles, daypart traffic counts, unit-level sales, the owner's basis, capital gains bracket and outstanding debt. The user supplied only a 2030–2035 expiry window.

Impact. These inputs determine the asset's income and residual value. Their absence is the largest limitation of the study.

What was done. Every one is listed as a blank row on the Site-Specific Variables page with a flag. The model was rebuilt to be scale-invariant so that the sell-versus-renew ranking does not depend on them.

Does this affect the recommendation? No. The ranking is driven by tax treatment and remaining lease term, neither of which depends on the parcel.

No single source URL resolves this item; it requires information not in the public record.

F-02 medium Unverified — conflicting sources

Los Angeles County 2030 and 2035 population projections

What was found. The official series is the California Department of Finance P-2A county total population file, Vintage 2026, released 13 March 2026. The workbook was not parsed during this build, so single-year county values for 2030 and 2035 remain blank. Two secondary sources disagree: 9,727,305 (The Basin, Aug 2026) and 9,867,515 (LA Almanac, undated, earlier vintage).

Impact. Low. Both sources and the official narrative agree the direction is decline; only the magnitude differs, and the model uses the official K-12 enrollment projection and the official 2040/2060 endpoints as its population signal.

What was done. Blanked in the tables. Modelled population drag (A17) is set at −0.25%/yr, well inside the range implied by either source.

Does this affect the recommendation? No.

F-03 medium Unverified — retrieval failure

FEMA National Risk Index county score and disaster-declaration count

What was found. Several secondary sources state Los Angeles County has an NRI composite of 100.0/100 ('Very High', ranked first of California's 58 counties), with earthquake as the dominant hazard, and 88 federal disaster declarations since 1969. The FEMA NRI API path returned HTTP 404, and FEMA's own page states the standalone NRI application has been retired in favour of RAPT. An OpenFEMA DisasterDeclarationsSummaries filter on designatedArea returned an empty result set, which is a query artefact rather than evidence of zero declarations.

Impact. Moderate. The hazard direction is not in doubt — CAL FIRE's incident records and the assessment-roll wildfire relief both corroborate severe hazard — but the precise ranking cannot be cited.

What was done. Recorded as unverified and excluded from the data tables. Hazard conclusions rest on the CAL FIRE incident records (S21, S22) and the assessment roll (S25), both verified.

Does this affect the recommendation? No.

F-04 medium Unverified — retrieval failure

USGS 30-year earthquake probabilities for the Los Angeles region

What was found. The UCERF3 figures most often quoted are 60% for M6.7+ in the Los Angeles region over 2014–2043, and 93% for Southern California. Multiple secondary outlets repeat them consistently, but a usgs.gov page carrying the figures returned HTTP 404 during this session.

Impact. Low for the decision. Earthquake risk is real and unhedged, but its probability does not distinguish between the seven strategies — it applies identically to all of them.

What was done. Excluded from the tables and stated here. The seismic scenario is described qualitatively with an explicit note that its probability is unverified.

Does this affect the recommendation? No.

F-05 high Unverified — no official source exists

Capitalisation rates and commercial property price indices for Los Angeles

What was found. No official government series publishes net-lease cap rates for single-tenant quick-service retail, and no official Los Angeles commercial repeat-sales index was retrieved. NCREIF and CoStar publish credible data but are subscription products and are excluded by this study's source rules.

Impact. High. The going-in cap rate is the single largest driver of every result.

What was done. The cap rate is declared as an explicit ASSUMPTION (A01, 5.75%, range 5.00–6.50%) built up from the verified 10-year Treasury plus a stated risk spread, not presented as a market observation. The sensitivity table shows it is the largest single swing factor. The user can replace it with an appraisal or broker opinion and the model recomputes.

Does this affect the recommendation? No — but it changes the magnitude of every number. See the sensitivity table.

F-06 high Unverified — personal financial data

Owner's tax basis, marginal rates and property tax base year value

What was found. Not available by design. The owner's adjusted basis, holding period, recapture position, applicable federal and California marginal rates, and assessed base year value determine the entire cost of selling.

Impact. Very high on the sell-versus-hold comparison, low on the ranking of structures.

What was done. Exposed as scenario parameters (A10 basis at 40% of value, A11 blended tax at 33.4%) with published ranges of 15–80% and 23.8–37.1%. Sensitivity analysis reports the swing.

Does this affect the recommendation? No. Even at the most favourable basis for selling, the exchange structures dominate.

I-02 medium Irregularity — statutory

The City of Los Angeles transfer tax exceeds the Revenue and Taxation Code ceiling

What was found. RTC §11911(b) allows a city within a taxing county to impose one-half the county amount — $0.275 per $500. The City of Los Angeles imposes $2.25 per $500 (0.45%), roughly eight times that figure. This is consistent with charter-city authority rather than §11911(b), but the statute alone does not explain the rate.

Impact. The city tax is a real cost and is used in the model. The statutory basis should be confirmed before relying on an arithmetic estimate in negotiation.

What was done. Both the statute (S16) and the City's own FAQ (S17) are cited. The model uses the City's published 0.45%.

Does this affect the recommendation? No.

I-04 low Irregularity — presentation

The 2026 assessment roll summary contains an internal error

What was found. The Assessor's roll announcement, as reproduced, states both '4.42% increase' and '42% increase over 2025 or an increase of $96 billion'. The $96 billion and the 4.42% are mutually consistent with a $2.272 trillion roll; '42%' is a typographical error in the distributed text.

Impact. None. The model uses only the 4.42% and the dollar components.

What was done. Only internally consistent figures are used. The error is noted so the reader is not confused by the same discrepancy in the press.

Does this affect the recommendation? No.
