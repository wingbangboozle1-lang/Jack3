Questions & evidence gaps

The questions this decision has to answer, sorted by whether an official source answers them, answers part of them, or cannot answer them at all. Every open item states exactly what would close it.

How to read this page. Answered means an official page settles it. Partial means official evidence covers part of the question and a private fact is still required. Open means no official source can answer it and a private document is required. Unverified means the item cannot be confirmed, so it is left blank and never inferred. Nothing on this page is a forecast, a valuation or advice.

Q-01DecisionWhat decision is actually being made, and by when?Either sell the fee interest before the lease term ends, or renew or extend and keep collecting rent. The reported expiry window 2030-2035 means the first date the owner can be forced to act is 2030, about 4 years from this snapshot, and the last is 2035, about 9 years out. Every route in the model is therefore evaluated on horizons of 5 to 9 years.AnsweredNo further input needed; the window is owner-supplied context, not a document (see Q-09).Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 · Q3 FY2026 results (quarter ended 2026-07-05) ↗
Q-03DecisionWhich single fact would most change the recommendation?A signed, enforceable extension of 10 years or more at market rent with acceptable credit. It converts a shortening income stream into a long one and widens the buyer pool, which is the only route that improves both the hold case and the exit case. It must be signed and guaranteed to count.AnsweredRequires the tenant's written terms (Q-13).Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 · Q3 FY2026 results (quarter ended 2026-07-05) ↗

Q-28RiskWhat happens if interest rates stay near current levels?The ten-year Treasury was 4.88% on 2026-11-09. Used as a discount-rate floor it is the most favourable column in the model: at a 4.88% required return holding beats selling even with an 18-month void, and the property needs only 1.33% rent growth to break even. The risk is not today's rate; it is a higher exit cap at the moment the owner sells.AnsweredNo further input needed; re-read FRED before acting, because the series is published daily.10-Year Treasury constant maturity yield (DGS10) ↗

Neither is published as passable to the landlord; in a NNN lease the labour cost lands on the tenant's margin, which feeds renewal willingness and closure risk.AnsweredThe lease's operating-covenant and co-tenancy clauses, if any.Fast Food Minimum Wage FAQ (AB 1228) ↗

Any one of them can move the answer, and none of them is public information today.AnsweredEach trigger is closed by one of Q-09 to Q-33.Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 · Q3 FY2026 results (quarter ended 2026-07-05) ↗
Q-02DecisionIs either route already ruled out by the evidence available today?Yes for one. Hold-to-expiry-and-do-nothing has no support. At the base assumptions holding to 2035 is worth 0.959 of selling today after costs, and 0.850 if an 18-month void occurs at expiry. A plan whose expected value is below the alternative and whose downside is a further 11% lower is not a plan. No route is ruled in, because the private documents are missing.PartialDepends on Q-09 to Q-14 (private documents).10-Year Treasury constant maturity yield (DGS10) ↗
Q-07MoneyWhat will it cost to sell?The statutory part is verified: City of Los Angeles base transfer tax 0.45% and county documentary transfer tax 0.11%; Measure ULA adds 4% above $5,400,000 and 5.5% at $10,900,000 or more for closings after 2026-06-30. Brokerage and diligence are not published by any official source, so the model uses a disclosed 2.0% assumption. At a 5.75% cap the all-in cost is 2.56% of value without ULA and 6.56% with the 4% ULA tier.PartialThe owner's listing agreement and whether the price crosses the ULA threshold.Real Property Transfer Tax and Measure ULA FAQ ↗
Q-08MoneyWhat will the owner actually keep after tax?Unknown, because the adjusted basis, the debt and the owner's marginal rates are private. What is verified: federal long-term capital gains are 0/15/20%, unrecaptured section 1250 gain is capped at 25%, California has no preferential capital-gains rate and taxes the gain as ordinary income, and a section 1031 exchange defers but does not eliminate the gain. The site publishes the tax drag as a function of assumed basis instead of guessing one.PartialDepreciation schedules, the acquisition closing statement, the current debt balance and prior-year returns: private.Topic no. 409 · Capital gains and losses ↗

Q-27RiskWhat happens to the owner's position if a catastrophe strikes before a sale?It is modelled, not predicted. That is why the recommendation caps this asset's share of net worth and requires insurance verification under either route.PartialInsurance verification (Q-25) and the owner's balance-sheet concentration (Q-30).The ShakeOut Scenario · USGS OFR 2008-1150 ↗
Q-29RiskWhat is the downside if the tenant defaults one year before expiry?Modelled at about -3.9% of the base hold case, before any guaranty recovery. Neither is known, so the number is illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast.PartialThe lease and guaranty, plus a re-letting estimate.Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1 · Q3 FY2026 results (quarter ended 2026-07-05) ↗
Q-33PersonalWould the owner qualify for, and want, a section 1031 exchange?Rules are verified: real property only, held for use in a trade or business or for investment and not held primarily for sale; replacement property must be identified within 45 days of the transfer and received within 180 days, or by the due date of the return including extensions, whichever is earlier; the exchange is reported on Form 8824; and the gain is deferred, not eliminated. Whether an exchange is available depends on the owner's facts and on finding a suitable replacement.PartialAdviser review of the owner's facts and an identified replacement candidate.Instructions for Form 8824 · Like-Kind Exchanges (2026) ↗
Q-34MoneyWhat is the county property-tax consequence of selling?Verified rules: the 1% general levy plus voter-approved add-ons, a 2% annual cap on assessed-value increases, reassessment on a change of ownership, and Proposition 8 temporary reductions on decline in value. A sale resets the base year, which can raise the buyer's tax and therefore lower the price they will pay, but the amount depends on the parcel's current assessed value, which is private.PartialThe current tax bill and the assessed value from the county assessor.Proposition 13 ↗
