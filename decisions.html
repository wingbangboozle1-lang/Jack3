What separates the routes

Every route that keeps the property keeps the whole regional hazard exposure, whatever the lease says. Only a sale, or an exchange into property outside the region, removes it. The routes differ in how long the exposure lasts and in how much room is left to react.

Best fit for preservation with manageable risk

Running both tracks in parallel carries the smallest avoidable risk: it costs effort and advisory fees, and in return a sale and an extension are both still available when the documents arrive. Holding to expiry and redevelopment sit at the other end.

Recommended sequence

Analyst judgement, not a valuation
  1. Now. Obtain the executed lease, amendments, estoppel, rent roll, tax bill, insurance policies and the owner's basis and debt — privately, never on this site.
  2. Now. Confirm jurisdiction, parcel hazard overlays and the remaining term including all renewal options.
  3. Plan timing. Consider confidential marketing and written renewal terms in parallel. The 24–36 month window is illustrative, not a verified deadline; lease notice rights may require earlier action.
  4. On receipt of both. Compute after-tax net sale proceeds against the discounted hold case using the verified rules on the Taxes page.
  5. Decision. Sell if the documented offer is acceptable; extend only against an enforceable extension when its risk-adjusted outcome better serves the owner.
  6. Either route. Verify insurance, size a re-letting reserve and cap this asset's share of Owner A's net worth at a level that can be lost without changing their plans.
