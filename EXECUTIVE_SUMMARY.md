# Executive summary — Asset A

**A Jack in the Box net-leased restaurant, central Los Angeles, NNN, lease expiring 2030–2035.**
*Evidence snapshot: 2026-11-10. Prepared by session `2aaf237e`. Anonymised: no address, parcel, owner or lease is published anywhere in this file or on the site.*

---

## 1. The question

The owner holds a single-tenant net-leased Jack in the Box in central Los Angeles with a lease reported to expire somewhere between 2030 and 2035. Should the property be sold before expiry, or should the lease be renewed — judged on the actual economic trajectory of the surrounding area, with a manageable level of risk and preservation of wealth taking priority over maximising upside?

## 2. The answer

**Open renewal negotiations now. If the tenant will commit to ten years or more at market rent, sell the lengthened lease. If it will not, sell before the expiry window rather than through it — preferably into a §1031 exchange.**

This is *analysis*, not a valuation. It is the conclusion that survives the worst case, which is the right test when the objective is preserving wealth. Specifically:

| | Pathway | Worst case | Expected | Best case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **D · Extend first, then sell** | **13.68 R** | **16.22 R** | 19.03 R |
| 2 | A · Sell now | 12.53 R | 14.76 R | 17.23 R |
| 3 | E · Re-tenant at expiry | 11.66 R | 15.66 R | 20.33 R |
| 4 | C · Seek renewal at expiry | 10.64 R | 15.89 R | 21.80 R |
| 5 | B · Hold to expiry, do nothing | 9.23 R | 13.47 R | 18.53 R |

*Values are present values in units of **R** = one year of current contractual base rent, because the actual rent was not supplied and will not be invented. Full assumptions: `model.html`.*

Extend-then-sell wins on the floor **and** on expected value. Holding to expiry and doing nothing — the default if nobody acts — ranks last on both.

## 3. Why — six findings that drive the decision

**1. The tenant is deliberately shrinking, and it has already said when it will close.** Jack in the Box guided to about 50–60 closures in FY2026 and closed 17 restaurants in a single quarter (Q3 FY2026, 4 openings against 17 closures, ending at 2,115 units). More important, its 2025 "JACK on Track" plan commits to closing a further 150–200 underperforming restaurants, with the remainder closing *"based upon respective franchise agreement termination dates."* A 2030–2035 lease expiry is precisely that kind of date.
→ [Q3 FY2026 earnings release, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/807882/000080788226000090/ex991-q326earningsrelease.htm) · [JACK on Track announcement](https://investors.jackinthebox.com/news/news-details/2025/Jack-in-the-Box-Inc--Unveils-JACK-on-Track-Plan-to-Improve-Long-Term-Financial-Performance/default.aspx)

**2. The tenant's income statement is contracting.** System same-store sales were −1.1% in Q3 FY2026, on top of −7.1% a year earlier. Franchise rental revenue — the line that is economically closest to the owner's position, since the issuer is itself a landlord — fell from $76.5m to $73.0m. The dividend and buyback are suspended and $500m of securitised notes were refinanced.
→ [Q3 FY2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/807882/000080788226000090/ex991-q326earningsrelease.htm)

**3. The regional economy is flat, not collapsing — and that is the whole point.** Metro unemployment fell to 4.4% from 5.5% and payrolls grew 0.5% year over year, led by education and health (+3.8%). But the county has 3.2% fewer residents than its 2020 estimates base, and the California Department of Finance projects a further 1.5% decline by 2035. Flat demand means no rent-growth tailwind to compensate for an expiring lease.
→ [BLS Los Angeles Area Economic Summary](https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/summary/blssummary_losangeles.pdf) · [Census QuickFacts, LA County](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/losangelescountycalifornia/PST045225) · [CA DOF P-2A projections](https://dof.ca.gov/media/docs/forecasting/Demographics/projections/P2A_County_Total.xlsx)

**4. The exit is taxed twice, and the city tax cliffs.** A City of Los Angeles sale above $5,400,000 pays a 4.45% city transfer tax (0.45% base plus the 4.00% Measure ULA tier), plus $0.55 per $500 county documentary transfer tax. The gain is then taxed federally (15/20% plus 3.8% NIIT) and again in California, which gives capital gains no preferential rate at all.
→ [City of LA Measure ULA FAQ](https://finance.lacity.gov/faq/measure-ula) · [RTC §11911](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=11911) · [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409) · [CA FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/personal/income-types/capital-gains-and-losses.html)

**5. Rates set the floor under any price a buyer will pay.** The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.86% on 2026-11-06, against 4.19% at the start of the year — an inherited model in this repository was still anchored to the old number. Every 100 bp of exit-yield expansion takes roughly 13% off the value of a constant income stream.
→ [FRED DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10)

**6. The tail risks are regional, not parcel-level, and none of them can be priced from public data.** USGS puts the Los Angeles region at a 60% chance of an M≥6.7 earthquake within 30 years, and models a plausible M7.8 San Andreas event at about 1,800 deaths and $213bn in losses. Two fires in January 2025 destroyed 16,264 structures in this county. For an urban infill pad the binding constraint after such an event is insurance availability, not the flame front — and no agency publishes an annual parcel-level probability for any of it.
→ [USGS UCERF3](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2015/3009/pdf/fs2015-3009.pdf) · [USGS ShakeOut](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1150/) · [CAL FIRE Eaton](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/eaton-fire) · [CAL FIRE Palisades](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/palisades-fire)

## 4. The risk balance

**Business as usual — the risks that are certain to be present**

| Risk | Evidence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Income concentration: one tenant, one building | Lease expiry 2030–2035, single obligor | Sell while income is contractual |
| Tenant closure at or before expiry | 50–60 closures guided; block programme to agreement dates | Get a written renewal answer early |
| Wage-floor compression on the franchisee | Fast-food floor $20.00/hr (AB 1228); City of LA $18.42 from 2026-07-01; state $16.90 | Ask for unit-level P&L in diligence |
| Re-letting risk | Purpose-built box, flat demand | Negotiate a rent floor, not a hope |
| Rate and exit-yield risk | 10-year Treasury 4.86% | Model at +100 bp |
| Transfer and gains tax | 4.45% city + county + federal + CA | Price the net, evaluate §1031 |

**Black swans — low frequency, high severity**

| Event | What the official record says | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Earthquake | 60% for M≥6.7 in the region within 30 years; M7.8 scenario ~1,800 deaths, $213bn | Building damage, business interruption, insurance repricing |
| Wildfire | 16,264 structures destroyed in LA County in January 2025 alone | Insurance withdrawal can force a sale regardless of the real-estate case |
| Flood / ground failure | FEMA and CGS are the official maps; the parcel zone is unknown | Undetermined exposure that cannot be priced from public data |
| Tenant insolvency | Dividend stopped, sales falling two years running, debt refinanced | Rent stops the day an operator fails, not the day the lease expires |

**How the recommendation manages that risk.** Extending first converts a wasting asset into a longer income stream, which is what a net-lease buyer actually purchases — and it does so without selling. If the tenant will not extend, the owner knows something decisive: the income has a hard end date and the tenant has already published its intention to close stores at agreement dates. Selling into that information, rather than through it, is the lower-risk path. The route that maximises nothing is the default: waiting.

## 5. What is deliberately blank

Nothing in this file was estimated. Twenty-one items are published blank on the site's [Flagged for review](flagged.html) page with the document that would close each one. The material ones:

- **The property's identity** — address, parcel, AIN. Withheld by design; privacy is the highest-priority requirement.
- **Rent, escalations, option terms, guarantor** — in the executed lease, which was not supplied.
- **Basis, holding period, debt, filing status** — owner records. Without them no after-tax figure about this property is computable.
- **Cap rates and sub-market vacancy** — no federal or state agency publishes them; commercial broker research was excluded by the official-sources rule.
- **Parcel-level flood, fire and liquefaction zones** — the FEMA, CAL FIRE and CGS tools are all address- or parcel-keyed.
- **Metro employment projection to 2035** — EDD publishes it only through an interactive Tableau application, and nothing was extracted.
- **Any dollar value for the property** — would require inventing a rent, an area and a cap rate.

## 8. What would change the answer

1. A signed extension at market rent for ten years or more — this *is* the recommended path; if the tenant agrees, execute it.
2. A verified after-tax bid above the hold case — a real offer beats a model.
3. Block-level evidence that this specific trade area is growing, which city and county averages cannot show.
4. A change in the owner's liquidity needs or time horizon.
5. A loan maturity inside the sale window, which can force the timing regardless of the real-estate case.

None of these can be established from public data. The first four require documents only the owner holds.
