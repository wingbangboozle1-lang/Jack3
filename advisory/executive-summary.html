Core Thesis: Why Passive Holding to 2030–2035 Carries Uncompensated Asymmetry

A triple-net (NNN) lease shields the landlord from property taxes, insurance, and maintenance only until the primary term ends. Once a QSR lease enters its final 3–5 years, commercial buyer financing tightens and the landlord absorbs 100% of continuation risk. Here, continuation risk is elevated by three verified facts: (1) Jack in the Box closed net 76 units between FY2024 and Q3 FY2026 under its 150–200 unit JACK on Track program while posting negative comps (−4.2% FY25, −1.1% Q3 FY26), (2) California's statutory $20.00/hr fast-food wage and +20.3% LA energy inflation compress store margins against a shrinking county population (−3.2% V2025), and (3) holding a single unhedged LA parcel across 2026–2035 retains cumulative exposure to USGS seismic risk (60% 30-yr M6.7+) and the Dec 31, 2028 sunset of California's high-value commercial FAIR Plan expansion.

Optimal Solution: Test an early 10–15 yr blend-and-extend first (Pathway P1); whether extended or declined (Pathway P2), exit before the short-fuse financing cliff via an IRC §1031 exchange to preserve 100% of tax-deferred principal while upgrading tenant and geographic diversification.
