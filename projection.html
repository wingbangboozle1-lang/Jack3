The projection system

A seeded Monte Carlo engine written in the Python standard library. It separates three things that are normally blended together and thereby hidden: observations (official data, cited), assumptions (modelling judgement, labelled and ranged), and outputs (computed, reproducible). Re-running python3 tools/model.py reproduces every figure below exactly, because the random seed is fixed.

The model is deliberately scale-invariant: current rent is expressed as a going-in cap rate on value, so nothing depends on knowing the property's price, rent or basis. Three value bands are run because they straddle the two Measure ULA thresholds.

On a calm path, waiting always pays — the landlord keeps earning, and rent grows at 2.75% against cost inflation of nothing. That is precisely why the calm path is misleading. The last column is what waiting actually costs: cumulative probability of at least one shock, rising from 0% at an immediate sale to 16.6% by 2035. The model's whole purpose is to price that column, and it is why the recommendation pairs a renewal with a planned exchange date rather than an open-ended hold.

7. What the model cannot see

  • Contract rent, basis and lease terms (F-01, F-06) — exposed as scenario parameters instead.
  • Whether the tenant has already decided to close this unit (F-01).
  • Parcel-level hazard: fire hazard severity zone, flood zone, seismic construction type (F-01, F-03).
  • Trade-area population, traffic counts and zoning — the true demand and residual-value drivers.
  • The existence of an actual comparable sale. Neither a broker opinion nor an appraisal was available.

Each of these is a row in the data tables with a blank value, not an estimate.
